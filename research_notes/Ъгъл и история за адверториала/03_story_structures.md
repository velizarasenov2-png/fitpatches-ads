## Q1. Which story archetypes dominate winning health/weight-loss advertorials and VSLs, with named examples, and which one fits a Bulgarian woman aged 35–60 best?

### Takeaway
Almost every winning health story reduces to one spine: **"someone like you (or someone you love) was in pain → hit a breaking point → went looking for the REAL cause → found a hidden/contrarian explanation → found a simple fix."** The archetypes differ only in *who* suffers and *where* the discovery comes from. The classic ClickBank variants (loved one near death, secret from a far-off island, whistleblower vs. industry) are now strongly associated with scams and fake people. For Bulgarian women 35–60 the best fit is **"an ordinary woman like me, whom nobody believed, finds out it wasn't her fault"**, a peer story with family witnesses and no exotic or doctor hero.

### Cited Findings
**The common spine (RMBC and classic DR)**
- Stefan Georgi's RMBC sales-letter skeleton has 7 parts: Lead → Background Story → Unique Problem Mechanism → Unique Solution Mechanism → Product Buildup & Reveal → Close → FAQs. — [RMBC overview (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/document/622267942/Stefan-copy-structure)
- RMBC Background Story, verbatim: the spokesperson "or someone close to the spokesperson… used to be just like the reader… In pain and looking for solutions… But then they hit a breaking point, and it led them on a quest to find the REAL cause of their problem." — [Stefan Georgi, lead checklist](https://www.stefanpaulgeorgi.com/blog/heres-my-checklist-for-writing-a-killer-lead/)
- RMBC lead checklist (order flexible): call out problem; promise solution; **"Tease Emotional Discovery Story"**; tease the unique mechanism; **"Tease Contrarian Nature of Mechanism (This Flies in the Face of What You've Been Told Before)"**; fascinations; "Briefly Address Skepticism"; credibility builders; qualifiers; broad testimonials. An emotional lead teases the dramatic story element *without resolving it* in the lead. — [Stefan Georgi](https://www.stefanpaulgeorgi.com/blog/heres-my-checklist-for-writing-a-killer-lead/)
- Evaldo Albuquerque (16-Word Sales Letter): "The secret to converting copy is to define the one belief, then answer these ten questions." The one belief links the opportunity to the prospect's desire through a mechanism, and the story serves that belief. — [Goodreads quotes](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/72898982-the-16-word-sales-letter-a-proven-method-of-writing-multi-million-doll); [dannybooboo summary](https://dannybooboo.com/books/16-word-sales-letter-book-review-notes-summary-evaldo-albuquerque/)
- Masterson & Forde, *Great Leads*: six lead types (Offer, Promise, Problem-Solution, **Secret**, Proclamation, **Story**). They sit on an awareness continuum, direct for informed and eager prospects, indirect (Story, Secret) for cold and skeptical ones. Secret lead rule: the secret is "Introduced in the headline… Not disclosed in the lead". "The longer you can get your prospect mesmerized by a product that's hidden, the greater your chance it will close." — [Samuel J. Woods summary](https://samueljwoods.com/great-leads-masterson-forde/); [Write With AI on Great Leads](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/great-leads-kickstart-part-1-the)

**Archetype 1: "Ordinary person discovers a secret" [historical, still the backbone]**
- Gary Halbert, *"The Amazing Diet Secret Of A Desperate Housewife"* (late 1970s). It opens "My name is Nance Pryor. I am 35 years old. I am a housewife and a mother of three children. I would like to tell you something personal about myself." The ad builds rapport before any pitch, dramatises a rock-bottom moment "very specific at depicting the crushing moment", then an epiphany: a solution she "never dreamed existed", after which "all the painful moments mentioned before have stopped." — [Jake Liikanen breakdown](https://jakeliikanen.substack.com/p/breakdown-of-a-fitness-ad-from-the). A companion book by Nancy Pryor was published in 1978 — [Etsy listing, 1978 1st ed.](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1812099183/the-amazing-diet-secret-of-a-desperate)
- Note: the current FitPatches draft opens with almost the same formula ("Казвам се Даниела. На 47 съм, от Пловдив, счетоводителка и майка на две деца.").

**Archetype 2: "Loved one's crisis" + Archetype 3: "Discovery abroad" + "Us vs. them" [historical ClickBank]**
- *Halki Diabetes Remedy*: the copy "drops readers straight into a drama-spiked, life-or-death scene where the protagonist's wife is minutes away from a potentially fatal diabetic coma". The story then follows a hero's-journey quest with "insider whistleblowers pulling the lid off the 'diabetes industry'" and a cure found on a tiny Greek island. — [J.L. Torrance, "Sales Letter Showdown" (Medium)](https://medium.com/@jltorrancecopy/sales-letter-showdown-first-match-8fef77b0759f) (page returned 403; quoted from the search snippet)
- The Halki story uses a hyper-specific number: the wife's blood sugar "of 488", plus a doctor who had never "seen it this bad before". — [Yahoo Finance-syndicated review](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/halki-diabetes-remedy-reviews-working-230000008.html)
- Review sites allege that spokesman "Eric Whitfield" doesn't exist and that his photo is a stock image. — [Contra Health Scam](https://www.contrahealthscam.com/halki-diabetes-remedy-review-scam/); [Leah's Fitness](https://www.leahsfitness.com/halki-diabetes-remedy-review-scam-by-eric-whitfield/). San Diego CAN calls it "baloney" — [SDCAN](https://sandiegocan.org/2019/04/22/scam-alert-halki-diabetes-cure-is-baloney/)
- *Okinawa Flat Belly Tonic* (≈2020–21) combines a loved one's humiliation with a discovery abroad. The narrator's sister Susan attends her son Jordan's award ceremony. Asked who his mom is, Jordan points: "she is that big lady over there." The fix comes from a Japanese cardiologist, "Dr. Tamaki". — [IPS News review](https://ipsnews.net/business/2020/12/23/okinawa-flat-belly-tonic-supplement-review-7-undeniable-facts/); [Mike Banner "history" page](https://okinawa-flat-bellytonic.com/mike-banner-history-creator-of-okinawa-flat-belly-tonic-supplement/)
- *Exipure* (launched Oct 2021) was framed as a "tropical loophole" from a "Vietnamese weight loss specialist Dr. Vincent Lam", presented by spokesperson "Jack Barrett". — [Washington City Paper (sponsored review)](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/551565/exipure-review-guide-based-on-customer-reviews-ofbrthe-trending-tropical-weight-loss-supplement-of-2022/)
- *Red Tea Detox* (top of ClickBank gravity in June 2018) was built on an exotic "African Red Tea" that critics identify as ordinary rooibos. — [SDCAN](https://sandiegocan.org/2019/07/10/scam-alert-red-tea-detox-is-dangerously-deceitful/); [CBEngine](https://cbengine.com/id/redteax)
- "Most top performing ClickBank promos open with a story, because people are hardwired to listen to stories." — [Dean Mackenzie](https://dean-mackenzie.com/clickbank-marketing-secrets/) (search snippet; the page body would not load)

**Archetype 4: "Nightmare story" (a rock-bottom variant, Deutsch/Goff)**
- Justin Goff learned the "nightmare" story from David Deutsch: "Most people with digestive issues have some type of horror story that they're scared to death might happen again." His email followed one: golfing with friends → sudden digestive emergency → no bathroom → running to the woods. It "ran successfully on cold traffic for nearly six months." A good nightmare story has "shame and embarrassment dripping out" of it. "Every market has nightmare stories like this." — [Justin Goff, Part 2](https://www.justingoff.com/part-2-this-type-of-story-always-works-in-copy/)
- Goff contrasts charity letters that rattle off stats ("over 4 million of them") with personal stories: "nothing grabs people's attention and moves them more than an emotional story." — [Maximum Lawyer podcast ep. 210](https://maximumlawyer.com/copywriting-w-justin-goff-210/)

**Archetype 5: Doctor-led / expert interview (common in EU COD nutra)**
- EU COD prelanders typically use "celebrity stories, doctor/nutritionist, 'conspiracy' (what the industry hides), seasonal". — [Traffic Cardinal](https://en.trafficcardinal.com/post/no-pain-no-gain-investigating-weight-loss-offers) (via internal notes 03)
- A Romanian investigation found fake landing pages, stolen identities of doctors and celebrities, and stock-photo testimonials in the CEE nutra sector. — [AIN.UA on Everad](https://en.ain.ua/2021/03/17/everad-heads-massive-scam/) (via internal notes 03)

**Fit for the audience: evidence on similarity**
- A 2024 meta-analysis (19 articles, 123 effect sizes, including health topics) found that a character similar to the reader yields stronger identification (d = 0.14) and self-referencing (d = 0.16), marginally more transportation, and less resistance. "Similarity manipulation based on chosen demographic and biographic factors appeared to be more effective." — [Chen, Dong & Wang, *Communication Research* 51(1)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00936502231204834)
- Bulgarians report high trust in their own GP (74.9% high trust, mean 3.92/5) and in specialists (82.3%, 4.11/5), but low trust in hospitals (50.7%) and in the health system overall (4.1–4.4 on a 10-point scale, 2021–2024). — [PMC: Social and Institutional Trust in the Bulgarian Health System](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12555277/)

### Inferences
- **Archetype ranking for FitPatches BG, women 35–60:**
  1. **Peer "ordinary woman discovers it wasn't her fault"** (Halbert lineage + RMBC "quest for the REAL cause"). Best fit. The similarity meta-analysis supports a narrator who matches the reader's age, family role and job. "Not your fault" is also the Meta-safe emotional core: it relieves shame instead of creating it.
  2. **"Loved one" as witness or rescuer, not victim.** The daughter who turns on the light is a strong device. A "loved one near death" (Halki) is too melodramatic, implies medical claims, and points toward diabetes, which compliance notes flag as the most dangerous target.
  3. **"Skeptic converted"**, e.g., the mother-in-law who never believed and then experiences it herself (see Q6 concept C). Fresh, and it speaks to the 55–60 half of the audience.
  4. **Avoid:** discovery abroad or on an exotic island (the BG audience has seen these from scam networks), the "Д-р …" hero (fake-doctor scandals; Art. 246 ЗЛПХМ risk per internal compliance notes), and hard "us vs. them" against doctors (Bulgarians trust *their* GP; blaming "the system" or "nobody told us" is safer than villainising the doctor).
- The **contrarian element** can be scientific rather than conspiratorial: "it's not the spike, it's the *dip*" is already in the draft and is a good, credible contrarian twist ("This flies in the face of what you've been told: eat less").
- Use the Secret/Story lead (indirect) because Meta cold traffic is mostly problem-aware, not product-aware.

### Gaps
- I could not find a primary teardown of an "expert's own struggle" or "doctor who was wrong" weight-loss VSL (e.g., a surgeon-turned-diet-author story) with verifiable details, so that archetype is described from general practice only.
- No performance data (CTR/CVR) compares archetypes against each other; the "what works" claims are practitioner opinion plus ClickBank gravity, not controlled tests.
- Exipure's full VSL story (whether it has a loved-one beat) could not be verified; only the "Vietnamese specialist / tropical loophole" framing is sourced.
- The Medium Halki teardown returned 403; the quotes come from the search snippet.

## Q2. Beat by beat: how do top copywriters structure the story, and what are the lengths and proportions?

### Takeaway
The sourced skeleton is: **scene/hook → who I am → failed attempts → deeper wound → rock bottom → turn ("it's not your fault") → new mechanism → solution criteria → discovery of product → skepticism → small first win → (setback) → transformation and new identity → bridge to the reader → proof → offer.** The product should not appear for the first few hundred words. No authoritative source gives percentages per beat; the proportions below are a working model I derived from the sources and the 2,180-word draft.

### Cited Findings
- Advertorial page order (2026 practitioner guide): (1) story or education open, "No product mention for the first few hundred words. This is where the reframe from the ad's hook gets room to land"; (2) mechanism, "Why the standard approach fails… the section doing the heaviest belief-building work"; (3) **solution criteria**, "What a real fix would need to do, stated before the product shows up"; (4) product "should feel inevitable rather than pitched"; (5) proof stack; (6) offer "placed last, after the argument is complete". — [AdLibrary: Advertorial Landing Pages](https://adlibrary.com/posts/advertorial-landing-pages)
- Halbert's beats [historical]: hook headline → personal intro and relationship building → specific rock bottom → "common approaches don't work" → epiphany ("never dreamed existed") → payoff: the painful moments stop. — [Jake Liikanen](https://jakeliikanen.substack.com/p/breakdown-of-a-fitness-ad-from-the)
- RMBC: Lead teases the emotional discovery story without resolving it → Background story (just like the reader → breaking point → quest for REAL cause) → UMP → UMS → product reveal → close. — [Stefan Georgi](https://www.stefanpaulgeorgi.com/blog/heres-my-checklist-for-writing-a-killer-lead/); [Scribd RMBC](https://www.scribd.com/document/622267942/Stefan-copy-structure)
- Nightmare-story micro-beats: setup (normal moment) → conflict → rising tension → crisis ("oh crap" moment) → forced action. — [Justin Goff](https://www.justingoff.com/part-2-this-type-of-story-always-works-in-copy/)
- Length: practitioner guides put most effective advertorials at roughly 800–2,500 words, with a "70% content / 30% sales" rule of thumb. — [Apexure guide 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/advertorials-a-detailed-guide-to-design-and-best-practices); [Native Advertising Institute](https://www.nativeadvertisinginstitute.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-advertorials). I saw these ranges only in search summaries and could not tie each figure to a specific page. Internal BG notes (05) separately recommend 1,500–2,500 words with skimmable subheads and 1–3-line paragraphs because ≈67% of traffic is on mobile.
- Write to one person: Goff's six-figure mistake was writing to a group ("We all know…", "How many of you…"); "copy should feel personal. It should feel like you're talking to someone 1-on-1." — [Justin Goff, Part 3](https://www.justingoff.com/part-3-a-copy-mistake-that-cost-me-6-figures/)

### Inferences

- **Rule of the open loop:** start at the rock bottom, cut away to the backstory, and return to resolve it. The draft tells the story chronologically, so the most gripping moment arrives only after a CV and a list.
- **Callback rule:** whatever image carries the rock bottom (dark kitchen, 21:15, daughter, light switch) must come back, transformed, in the payoff. The draft already does this; keep it.
- About **55–60% of the words** go to story + mechanism before the product appears (beats 1–10). That matches the "no product for the first few hundred words" rule and the 70/30 content/sales heuristic.

## Q3. Believability devices: what makes a story feel true, what makes it feel fake, and what narrative-persuasion research says

### Takeaway
Research on narrative transportation (Green & Brock; the van Laer 2014 meta-analysis) shows that **identifiable characters, an imaginable plot and verisimilitude** each raise transportation. Transportation in turn raises emotion and story-consistent beliefs and *reduces critical thoughts*. Perceived commercial intent suppresses it. In practice, believability comes from **specific, non-round details, admitted flaws, two-sided honesty, small and gradual wins, imperfect timelines, and side characters with their own voice**. Stories feel fake when they are too smooth, too fast, round-numbered, stock-persona'd, or full of medical heroes.

### Cited Findings
**Narrative-persuasion research**
- Green & Brock (2000), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*: "The role of transportation in the persuasiveness of public narratives". Transportation is a combination of attention, imagery and feelings. More-transported readers adopt more story-consistent beliefs, and transportation reduces counterarguing. The scale has 11 general and 4 imagery items. — [University at Buffalo record](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/the-role-of-transportation-in-the-persuasiveness-of-public-narrat/); [Study.com summary](https://study.com/academy/lesson/narrative-transportation-definition-application.html)
- van Laer, de Ruyter, Visconti & Wetzels (2014), *Journal of Consumer Research* 40(5): a meta-analysis of **132 effect sizes from 76 articles**. Storyteller-controlled antecedents: **identifiable characters (ρ = .20), imaginable plot (ρ = .29), verisimilitude (ρ = .27)**. Receiver factors: familiarity (.21), attention (.29), transportability (.30), education (.10), **(female) sex (.15)**, **age no effect (.00)**. Consequences of transportation: **affective responses ρ = .57 (large)**, attitudes .44, intentions .31, beliefs .26, narrative thoughts .20, and **critical thoughts ρ = –.20**. — [van Laer et al. 2014, City Univ. open-access PDF](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/6755/)
- Same paper: verisimilitude is "lifelikeness", the likelihood that story events *may* happen. It is distinct from being factual: "nonfictionality refers to… that which has happened, verisimilitude is about everything that may happen." Higher verisimilitude increases suspended reality. — [van Laer et al. 2014](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/6755/)
- Same paper, on commercial intent: when readers "realize a profit motive behind a story — its 'sponsored persuasion'… narrative transportation may be inhibited", and they generate critical thoughts "in response to stories that ooze with commercial interest". Proposition: "The more stories are noncommercial, the more narrative transportation increases." — [van Laer et al. 2014](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/6755/)
- Hoeken, Kolthoff & Sanders (2016), *Human Communication Research* 42(2): **story perspective** (whose eyes the story is told through) drives identification, and identification mediates attitude change. But when readers felt highly similar to the *opponent* character, that effect was blocked. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/hcre.12076)
- Similarity: d = 0.14 for identification and 0.16 for self-referencing; demographic and biographic similarity works best. — [Chen et al. 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00936502231204834)

**Two-sidedness and admissions**
- Eisend (2006) meta-analysis of two-sided advertising (217 effects): the overall persuasive effect is small but positive (r = .068), and effects on **source credibility are "rather strong"**. Admitting unfavourable information makes the source look more balanced. — [Eisend, *IJRM* 23(2)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167811606000267); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/240488833_Two-sided_advertising_A_meta-analysis)
- Halbert pre-empts the "I already know this" objection by saying the secret isn't "from the normal resources". — [Jake Liikanen](https://jakeliikanen.substack.com/p/breakdown-of-a-fitness-ad-from-the)
- RMBC lead includes "Briefly Address Skepticism". — [Stefan Georgi](https://www.stefanpaulgeorgi.com/blog/heres-my-checklist-for-writing-a-killer-lead/)

**Specificity and numbers**
- Halbert breakdown: "The more specific you can be, the better. Not only is it more likely that people will believe you but it also makes reading more interesting." — [Jake Liikanen](https://jakeliikanen.substack.com/p/breakdown-of-a-fitness-ad-from-the)
- Xie & Kronrod (2012, *Journal of Advertising*), four experiments: **numerical precision signals the advertiser's competence** and makes claims seem more factual. — summarised in [UCP thesis repository](https://repositorio.ucp.pt/bitstreams/db9884a0-d3a6-4dc5-bf30-144e3932b503/download); related work: ["It Seems Factual, But Is It? Sharp vs Round Numbers in Advertising Claims"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279544736_It_Seems_Factual_But_Is_It_Effects_of_Using_Sharp_versus_Round_Numbers_in_Advertising_Claims)
- Counterpoint: round numbers "activate associations of stability and increase perceived length of product benefits" (Pena-Marin & Bhargave 2016). — [*Journal of Consumer Psychology*](https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1016/j.jcps.2015.11.004)
- Halki used "blood sugar of 488" as a sharp-number device. — [Yahoo Finance review](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/halki-diabetes-remedy-reviews-working-230000008.html)

**What makes stories feel fake or backfire**
- Review sites alleging Halki's spokesman is a stock photo show how a persona gets "exposed". — [Contra Health Scam](https://www.contrahealthscam.com/halki-diabetes-remedy-review-scam/)
- Bulgarian red-flag education teaches readers to spot cloned news layouts, missing authors and dates, and stories that appear in only one outlet. — [Nauka.bg](https://nauka.bg/falshivite-lekari-onlain-raboti-izmamata-imitirani-medii-falshivi/) (via internal notes 05)
- Buyers arrive "carrying years of accumulated disappointment, a deeply conditioned distrust of weight loss marketing"; generic promises "trigger the distrust". — [Rob Palmer](https://robpalmer.com/blog/weight-loss-copywriting)

**Legal frame for a dramatised narrator (EU)**
- UCPD Annex I bans undisclosed paid editorial (pt 11), "falsely representing oneself as a consumer" (pt 22), and false or commissioned consumer reviews or endorsements (pts 23b/23c, added by Omnibus 2019/2161). Art. 7(2) makes it a misleading omission to fail to identify commercial intent. — [UCPD Annex I](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2005/29/annex/I); [EUR-Lex 2019/2161](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32019L2161&rid=2) (verified in internal notes 04)
- Cosmetics claims (Reg. 655/2013): "Marketing communications shall not imply that expressions of opinions are verified claims unless the opinion reflects verifiable evidence." — [Reg 655/2013 Annex](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2013/655/annex)

### Inferences
**Believability devices checklist (with FitPatches-ready examples)**
1. **Sharp, odd details instead of round ones:** times ("21:15", not "вечер"), prices ("2,40 € за кашкавала"), counts ("третата кутия капсули"). The draft already uses 21:15 well. Avoid round results ("−10 кг", "30 дни").
2. **Brand-free physical props:** cheese eaten from the packet, the open fridge as the only light, a sticky note on the fridge, the office biscuit tin, a salad "без олио", a cup of linden tea (липов чай). Specific objects drive imagery, which is the "imaginable plot" lever (ρ = .29).
3. **Dialogue from side characters with their own voice**, and at least one who is *not* a villain (husband teasing kindly, daughter worried, colleague casual). Per Hoeken 2016, a cartoon antagonist blocks identification for readers who resemble that antagonist. Some 55–60-year-old readers *are* свекърви.
4. **Admissions and flaws** (two-sided): "Ще бъда честна", "Пробвах и капсули — изкарах 10 дни", "Не е магия: ако вечерям торта, лепенката няма да ме спаси", a forgotten patch day, a slip at a birthday. Eisend shows two-sidedness raises source credibility.
5. **Modest, slow, behavioural wins** before any physical change: "Ден 1 — нищо." (the draft has this; good).
6. **An imperfect timeline:** a setback week, a "didn't notice anything until my sister said…" moment, a non-linear realisation.
7. **Interior monologue at the worst moment** ("Значи наистина съм слаба. Значи проблемът съм аз.") makes the scene feel lived in. Keep it in the narrator's voice; never address the reader's body.
8. **Delay commercial intent:** van Laer says sponsored persuasion inhibits transportation, so keep FitPatches out of the first ~50% of the page and keep the masthead and editorial voice sober. The "Рекламна публикация" label must stay (legal), so earn transportation through scene quality.
9. **Disclose the dramatisation without killing it:** the draft's top note ("Разказът на Даниела е драматизация, събрана от истории, които клиентки споделят с нас") is legally prudent and reads honestly. Make sure "истории, които клиентки споделят" is literally true (keep the source emails or reviews on file); otherwise reword. Never attach a real-looking photo of "Даниела" without "Илюстративно изображение (AI)". The draft does label its images.
10. **Fake-signals to avoid:** stock-perfect personas ("Мария, 52, от София, загуби 14 кг"), round kilo results, instant change ("още на третия ден"), all-positive diaries, a doctor saviour, exotic origins, a comment section full of 5-star "реални отзиви" placeholders (the draft currently has "Реален отзив…" placeholders; they must be replaced with verified reviews or removed).

### Gaps
- I found no study that directly tests *labelled dramatisations* versus real testimonials in health advertorials (conversion or trust). Whether "this is a dramatisation" lowers transportation is untested as far as I found.
- The Xie & Kronrod finding is from green-claim ads; I found no replication in health or diet stories.
- I did not verify the figures the draft cites from Wyatt et al. 2021 (*Nature Metabolism*, 1,070 participants, "big dippers", 9%, 312 kcal). Another researcher or the copy team should check them against the paper.

## Q4. Emotional payoff: which end-states convert best (identity, family, being seen) vs. kilo results, especially under Meta rules?

### Takeaway
Practitioners agree that the real sale in weight loss is **identity restoration and relief**, not the number on the scale. Meta rules (no negative self-perception, no implied knowledge of the viewer's body, no before/after, no results in a timeframe) push the same way. The strongest payoff mirrors the rock bottom and resolves the **deeper wound**. For Даниела that means being calm around food *and* being believed and exonerated, shown through family. Kilos should be absent or a quiet footnote.

### Cited Findings
- "The desire to lose weight is not primarily about weight. It is about identity." What prospects want is "the person who has energy to play with their children… who walks into a room feeling good rather than hoping no one looks at them… the person who feels like themselves again." Copy must paint "a vivid, specific picture of the identity transformation — not the number on the scale, but the life that becomes available." — [Rob Palmer](https://robpalmer.com/blog/weight-loss-copywriting)
- The real emotional sale is "the restoration of identity — the return of the self the prospect feels they have lost." — [Rob Palmer (search snippet)](https://robpalmer.com/blog/weight-loss-copywriting)
- Halbert's payoff structure: thanks to the solution, "all the painful moments mentioned before have stopped". — [Jake Liikanen](https://jakeliikanen.substack.com/p/breakdown-of-a-fitness-ad-from-the)
- Meta Personal Health policy: ads "must not imply or attempt to generate negative self-perception in order to promote diet, weight loss, or other health related products". — [Meta Business Help: Personal Health](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939)
- 2025–2026 enforcement: no before/after for weight loss; no phrasing like "Fix your flaws"; weight-loss and supplement ads limited to 18+. — [Adamigo: Meta 2025 health ad updates](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/metas-health-ad-policy-updates-what-changed). Personal-attribute "you" framing ("Lose your belly fat") is prohibited, and enforcement in 2026 catches indirect implications. Unverified weight-loss timeframes or amounts are banned. The on-policy reframe is to "spotlight the food, product, or environment — never the viewer's body". — [Primores wiki: Meta ad policy 2026](https://primores.org/wiki/marketing/meta-ad-policy/)
- Rate or amount of weight-loss claims ("−12 кг за 30 дни") are banned for foods, unsubstantiated for cosmetics, and "clickbait… specific outcomes within a set timeframe" on Meta. — internal notes 04 citing [Reg 655/2013](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2013/655/annex) and Meta policy

### Inferences
**Payoff menu, ranked for this audience and these rules:**
1. **Calm and freedom from "food noise"** ("Мамо, изглеждаш спокойна"). Already in the draft and excellent. It is body-neutral and relational, and it resolves the binge scene.
2. **Exoneration and being believed.** This resolves the *deeper wound* ("никой не ми вярваше"), which the draft sets up but never pays off. Add one beat where someone who doubted her understands, or where she understands herself: "Не съм била аз. Не е била волята ми." Ideally the husband, the daughter or the свекърва says a line that reverses "Просто яж по-малко", e.g., the husband after reading the study: „Значи не си била ти…"
3. **Belonging at the family table (inverse of the rock bottom).** The rock bottom is "salad alone at Sunday lunch → binge at night". A mirrored payoff is "Sunday lunch, I ate a сарма like everyone else, and that night I didn't open the fridge." It shows normal life, not a new body, and is very Bulgarian.
4. **Energy and presence:** "Вечер имам сили да изляза с дъщеря ми до Главната" (Plovdiv). Identity through activity, not shape.
5. **Ritual and self-respect:** "За първи път от години спазвах нещо седем дни подред." This is the self-efficacy payoff and is already in the draft.
6. **Kilos/scale:** demote to an unquantified side note, and avoid "Месец 2 — кантарът тръгна надолу" phrasing, which implies a weight result in a timeframe. Safer: "По някое време спрях да се тегля всеки ден. Дрехите ми го казаха преди кантара." Even this alludes to body change, so keep it off the Meta ad and use it sparingly on the page with the individual-results disclaimer.
- **Avoid as payoff:** swimsuit/beach "finally in a bikini", "my husband looks at me again", "the свекърва finally shut up". These are body-validation or revenge, risk negative self-perception and alienate older readers. Use the sea only as presence ("на морето бях във водата с децата, а не на хавлията с телефона"), and only on the page, never in the ad.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled data (A/B tests) comparing identity or relationship payoffs with result-number payoffs in weight-loss advertorials; the ranking is practitioner consensus plus policy constraints.
- I did not verify how strictly Meta reviews *landing-page* story text (vs. ad creative) for "negative self-perception"; sources describe ad creative as the primary enforcement point and landing pages mainly through claims and account-level risk.

## Q5. Bulgarian cultural story elements that make the story feel local and true, and the clichés to avoid

### Takeaway
The most truthful Bulgarian levers are not national symbols but **micro-rituals of family food pressure**: the maternal "Яде ли?", the double bind "I made your favourite, but don't eat much, you've put on weight", jars sent from mum, Sunday lunch at the свекърва's, office treats for name days, and a GP with ten minutes and a queue. Bulgarian women also face harsher weight judgement than men. Postcard props (шопска + ракия, кисело мляко, Тепетата, Слънчев бряг) and the "monster свекърва" read as clichés.

### Cited Findings
- "Яде ли???" is called the quintessence of Bulgarian maternal care (anxiety + devotion + control). A mother tells her slim daughter: **„Направила съм ти любимото ядене, ама не яж много, че си напълняла"**, then a month later sends heavy dishes. Mothers keep feeding adult children with **„омазнени торбички, пълни с буркани сарми и мусака"**. The article ties overfeeding to guilt and to food as connection and control. — [Жената днес, „Папкай, чеденце"](https://www.jenatadnes.com/malkiyat-prints/papkai-chedentse/)
- A search summary of the same family of sources says that in traditional rural Bulgarian culture buttering bread for children was obligatory and a mother who doesn't "fatten up" her child is seen as a bad mother. — [Жената днес](https://www.jenatadnes.com/malkiyat-prints/papkai-chedentse/) (summary-level; treat as cultural commentary)
- A Bulgarian feminist blog (2009) argues overweight women face marginalisation at work, socially and romantically while men don't. It cites a statistic of 52% of men vs 46% of women with excess weight, yet only women are ridiculed. It describes contradictory diet advice („яж белтъчини и ще се прецакаш. Недей да ги ядеш и пак ще се прецакаш") and traditional cuisine of „мазни, солени и сладки храни" alongside thin beauty ideals. — [OpenlyFeminist, „Дебелите жени"](https://openlyfeminist.com/2009/03/03/%D0%B4%D0%B5%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8/) (opinion blog, 2009; statistic unverified)
- Mother-in-law (свекърва) friction is a mainstream topic: common problems listed include controlling, critical comparisons, intrusiveness and judgement („Ако работите, това означава, че не отделяте достатъчно внимание на семейството си"). — [Tedko.bg](https://tedko.bg/%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B3/6-obichaini-problema-v-obshtuvaneto-sas-svekarvata-i-kak-da-se-spravite-s-tjah); forum threads such as „Каква е причината да не се разбираме със свекървата?" — [BG-Mamma](https://m.bg-mamma.com/?topic=213238.-1%3Ball). The "monster" framing is itself a media trope: „Тъщи, свекърви и други чудовища". — [Webcafe](https://webcafe.bg/onya-deto-ne-go-triyat/tashti-svekarvi-i-drugi-chudovishta.html)
- Sunday family lunch is a named ritual („Неделен обяд за цялото семейство") — [BG Fermer](https://www.bgfermer.bg/Article/11265131). Seasonal Sunday dishes such as „неделни пълнени чушки с кайма" from the last sweet peppers of autumn — [Burgas Media](https://burgasmedia.com/news/poslednite-sladki-chushki-nedelni-palneni-chushki-s-kayma)
- Real voice of customer: BG-Mamma thread titled **„Не ям, не ям, а не отслабвам"** (2008), about strict dieting plus daily exercise without results, including post-pregnancy weight — [BG-Mamma](https://www.bg-mamma.com/index.php?topic=290217) (title and summary only; the page returned 403). Similar threads: „Не мога да отслабна", „Не отслабвам, а пълнея" — [BG-Mamma](https://www.bg-mamma.com/?topic=36460); [BB-Team](https://www.bb-team.org/forums/viewthread/44603)
- GP trust is high (74.9%) and specialist trust higher (82.3%), but trust in hospitals and the system is low. — [PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12555277/). All insured Bulgarians choose a GP (личен лекар). — [PMC 2024 utilisation survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11293837/)
- Language: respectful Вие-form; a warm first-person monologue ("Бях на 47 и мислех, че просто такава ми е съдбата…") suits BG narrator advertorials (internal notes 05). Fake media, fake doctors and celebrity misuse are known scam signals in BG ([Nauka.bg](https://nauka.bg/falshivite-lekari-onlain-raboti-izmamata-imitirani-medii-falshivi/), via notes 05).

### Inferences
**Use these: specific, true-to-life Bulgarian elements**

| Element | How to use it in the story | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Double bind from family** | Lunch: свекърва „Само салата ли? Обиждаш ме. Хапни си от чушките, специално за теб ги правих." Evening: мъжът „Ти пък уж нищо не ядеш…" | More truthful than "everyone told me to eat less"; matches the documented „любимото ядене, ама не яж много" paradox |
| **Sunday lunch at the свекърва's** | One dish, specific and seasonal (October: пълнени чушки, свинско с праз, тиква с орехи), not a list | Imageable; a family-pressure arena |
| **Буркани / зимнина** | „Мама ми изпрати три буркана лютеница и сарми в тенджера" or a weekend печене на чушки за лютеница (October) | Love as food; seasonal autumn truth |
| **Office черпене for имен ден/рожден ден** | The 11:00 баница/пасти on the desk on Димитровден (26 Oct), Никулден, Ивановден; the colleague who *didn't* reach for it → discovery hook | Very real BG workplace ritual; makes the colleague discovery causal (see Q6) |
| **Quick breakfast culture** | „Баничка от фурната на ъгъла и кафе от машината" as the morning "spike" | Real, unbranded, explains the rollercoaster |
| **Личната лекарка: rushed, not wrong** | „Лекарката ми имаше десет минути и пълна чакалня. Каза: „Просто яжте по-малко" и ми написа направление за изследвания." | Bulgarians trust their GP; blaming time and the system matches low system trust |
| **Places as texture, not postcards** | Plovdiv districts (Тракия, Кършияка, Смирненски), "по Главната", "Капана" for an evening walk in the payoff | Local verisimilitude without tourist imagery |
| **Family milestones as future-pacing** | Son's or daughter's абитуриентски бал, a cousin's wedding, кръщене, "сватбата на племенницата през юни" | Big Bulgarian family events with a food table. Use as *presence* ("искам да танцувам на бала на сина си"), never as a weight deadline |
| **Holidays** | Бъдни вечер (fasting dishes), Коледа/Нова година, Великден (козунак), Сирни заговезни | Natural "slip and recover" setback beats |
| **Small spoken phrases** | „Хапни си, бе", „Яде ли?", „айде стига", „Мамо?…" | One or two per story; more becomes caricature |

**Avoid these clichés**
- The **triad "сарми, баница, торта"** in one breath (the draft has it): reads like a list of "Bulgarian foods" written for tourists. Pick *one* dish and give it a specific detail.
- Шопска салата + ракия, "българското кисело мляко", розово масло, "баба от село с билки" (folk-remedy cliché now linked to scam prelanders).
- Tourist geography: Тепетата/Античният театър as backdrop, "Слънчев бряг по бански".
- The **monster свекърва** and the **stupid doctor**: they alienate older readers (who are свекърви) and readers who trust their GP.
- Stock persona formulas from nutra prelanders: "Иванка, 52, от Стара Загора, свали 17 кг", "Д-р … от Александровска", "професор от БАН".
- Heavy regional dialect (Plovdiv „майна") or heavy slang: too cute for a dramatisation and it reads as parody.
- Calling the body „тлъста/дебела". The BG source shows how loaded „Тлъстото" is; keep the self-talk about weakness and will („слаба съм", „проблемът съм аз"), not about the body.

### Gaps
- I found no academic source on Bulgarian office "черпене" or name-day culture, the абитуриентски бал as a mother's milestone, or autumn лютеница-making as family ritual. These are well-known cultural facts but are listed here as inferences without citations.
- BG-Mamma thread bodies returned 403, so no verbatim post-level VOC quotes were captured beyond thread titles; a manual VOC pass (BG-Mamma, Facebook groups for menopause and weight) is recommended.
- The 52%/46% overweight statistic in the 2009 blog is unverified and dated.

## Q6. Critique of the current "Даниела" draft, and 2–3 alternative story concepts that could beat it

### Takeaway
The draft is **better than most nutra stories**. It has a real VOC headline, a sharp-time rock bottom (21:15, cheese from the packet, daughter turns on the light), a not-your-fault turn, solution criteria, honest pacing ("Ден 1 — нищо"), a body-neutral callback payoff ("Мамо, изглеждаш спокойна"), and visible dramatisation and AI-image labels. Its weaknesses are **structural and logical**:
- It opens with a CV instead of the scene.
- The deeper wound (disbelief) is never paid off.
- Everyone says "eat less", which is less true to Bulgarian life than a double bind.
- "I wasn't hungry" contradicts the hunger mechanism.
- The colleague discovery is a coincidence that arrives after she has already found berberine.
- The diary is too smooth.
- "Month 2: the scale went down" is a timeframe result.

### Cited Findings
- The draft's own text (visible-text extraction): opens „Казвам се Даниела. На 47 съм, от Пловдив, счетоводителка и майка на две деца."; the rock-bottom line „Не бях гладна. Бях… като в транс."; the mechanism section says the dip makes the body send a strong „гладна съм" signal ("гладът им е с около 9% по-силен"); discovery „Тогава видях малкия кръг на рамото на колежката ми"; diary "Месец 2. Halki "drops readers straight into" the crisis scene — [Medium teardown](https://medium.com/@jltorrancecopy/sales-letter-showdown-first-match-8fef77b0759f)

#### A. What works (keep)

- **Rock-bottom scene:** a precise time (21:15), dark kitchen, the fridge light, cheese straight from the packet (a perfect unbranded prop), "като в транс", the daughter's „Мамо?… Добре ли си?", crying in the bathroom „не заради кашкавала". This is a textbook nightmare story with shame "dripping" (Goff/Deutsch).
- **Turn line** „Вината не е във волята Ви." and the contrarian "не скокът, а спадът".
- **Solution criteria** (4 conditions) before the product.
- **Format objection** (capsules in the drawer) is a credible, two-sided "I tried it and failed" beat.
- **Honest diary opener** "Ден 1. Нищо." and the self-efficacy win "за първи път от години спазвах нещо седем дни подред".
- **Callback payoff** "Мамо, изглеждаш спокойна. Спокойна. Не „по-слаба"." returns to the same daughter and kitchen. Strong, relational and Meta-friendly.
- **Disclosure** of dramatisation and AI images at the top and bottom.

**Is the colleague discovery strong enough?** Only partly. Peer discovery is believable in Bulgaria (word of mouth beats ads for this audience), and the visual „малкият кръг на рамото" is good. As written, though, it is a coincidence that arrives *after* the narrator has already done the research, so it adds little. It becomes strong if (a) the colleague is planted in the office scene before the rock bottom, (b) Daniela notices a behaviour change first (not reaching for the name-day баница at 11:00), and (c) the colleague is casual and non-salesy, with a small admission ("Не е магия. Просто вечер не ме тегли така").

**Is the rock bottom specific enough?** Yes. It is the strongest part. Two refinements: fix the "не бях гладна" contradiction, and tie it causally to the Sunday double bind ("салата на обяд → 21:15 кашкавал"), which makes the mechanism land later ("salad-only lunch = bigger dip").

**Is the payoff right?** Mostly. Keep "спокойна". Add **exoneration** (the deeper wound) and consider a **Sunday-table mirror** (eating a normal portion at the свекърва's table, with no fridge that night).

#### C. Alternative story concepts (loglines)

**Concept A: "Ревизията" (The Audit). Ordinary-person-investigator / skeptic.**
Даниела, 47, a Plovdiv accountant, has been told for years to "just eat less", and after the 21:15 fridge night she does the only thing she trusts: an audit. For 14 days she logs every bite and rates her hunger 1–10 in a spreadsheet, like a client's books. The numbers don't lie: on "good" days with a tiny lunch, the hunger spikes at 11:00 and 21:00 are *worse*. Her spreadsheet matches a study of 1,070 people on post-meal "dips". The fix she needs must pass her four audit criteria, and the patch is the only option that fits her mornings. Payoff: the audit she shows her husband ends the "you must be sneaking food" suspicion, and her daughter's "Мамо, изглеждаш спокойна".
*Why it could beat the draft:* the discovery is earned, the profession matters, there is a built-in reader micro-action („запишете часа три вечери") that drives self-recognition, and the exoneration is evidence-based. It fits the analytical, distrustful BG reader.

**Concept B: "Светнатата лампа" (The Light Switch). Loved one as witness and rescuer, two voices.**
Opens with the 16-year-old daughter's short paragraph: "Тази вечер светнах лампата в кухнята и видях мама…". Scared, the daughter searches „мама яде нощем пред хладилника" and finds the glucose-dip research. She brings it to her mother: "Мамо, не си ти. Виж." Mother and daughter work out the rollercoaster together (a teen-friendly explanation). The patch is found by the mother through a colleague or pharmacy, not the teen. The payoff is the daughter's line again, "изглеждаш спокойна", now earned by both.
*Why it could beat the draft:* the rock-bottom witness drives the discovery (causal, not coincidental), and the mother–daughter bond is the strongest relationship lever for 35–50-year-olds. *Risk:* keep the daughter away from diet or body talk (Meta and ethics); she finds *science*, not a slimming product.

**Concept C: "Свекървата" (The Mother-in-law Who Didn't Believe). Skeptic converted, speaking to the 55–60 half of the audience.**
Ганка, 61, retired teacher from Пазарджик, spent twenty Sunday lunches telling her daughter-in-law "Ти пък уж нищо не ядеш, как не отслабваш?". After menopause she finds *herself* at the fridge at 21:30, with the same shame. Her daughter-in-law shows her the dip research and the patch she has been quietly using. The story ends at a Sunday table: "Тая неделя пак направих пълнени чушки. Тя си хапна. И този път не ѝ казах нищо за чинията ѝ."
*Why it could beat the draft:* a fresh angle in a market full of 40-something narrators. It turns the antagonist into the protagonist (avoiding the Hoeken "similar-to-opponent" block for older readers), carries a reconciliation payoff, and makes cross-generational sharing natural (mother-in-law and daughter-in-law both become buyers). *Risk:* keep menopause references general and non-medical.

**Optional micro-variant (cheapest test): "Димитровден".** Keep Даниела but restage the discovery at the office name-day treats (26 Oct, timely for an October/November launch). The colleague who doesn't touch the баница at 11:00 becomes the noticed clue. This changes only ~200 words of the existing draft.

**Test recommendation (inference):** A/B the current draft (re-ordered to a cold open, with fixes 2–4 and 9) against Concept A. Use Concept C as a separate ad-set angle for 55–60 targeting. Measure scroll depth to the mechanism section and CTR to the offer, not only CPA.
