# Global benchmark: ingrown-hair pads for the bikini / pubic area, and gaps for Bulgaria

Research date: 6 October 2026. Amazon.com, Amazon.de, Amazon.co.uk and notino.bg search results were scraped live on 6 Oct 2026 with Firecrawl, so ratings, prices and "bought in past month" figures are a snapshot from that day. The Amazon.de and .co.uk scrapes were rendered for a US visitor, so some prices came back in USD rather than EUR or GBP. Older press data is labelled with its publication date. The "price per pad" figures are my own arithmetic (price ÷ pad count) on the cited prices.

## 1. Who leads the category, and which products sell best?

### Takeaway
The acid-pad format for ingrown hairs is led by **First Aid Beauty (FAB) Ingrown Hair Pads**. It is the clear best-seller on Amazon US (about 5.6K ratings and "3K+ bought in past month") and is the only branded pad with real distribution in Europe. Below it sit a cheap mass tier (Completely Bare at about $0.16–0.19 per pad, Base Laboratories, Viking Revolution) and Bushbalm's newer pad (Radiant Reset, 30 large pads). Several famous "ingrown" brands do **not sell pads at all**: Fur, European Wax Center, Gigi, PFB Vanish, Tend Skin, Malin+Goetz, Flamingo and Skin Doctors use oils, serums, roll-ons, lotions or patches. The biggest unit volume in the wider category on Amazon US is actually a **roll-on serum** (Peach Slices, "20K+ bought in past month"), not a pad.

## 2. Typical actives, concentrations, materials, counts and price per pad

### Takeaway
The proven formula is **salicylic acid (BHA) + glycolic acid (AHA)** in a witch-hazel and soothing base (aloe, green tea, licorice, bisabolol). Most brands **do not disclose percentages**. The one data point found is Bliss at about 4–5.9% glycolic and 1–1.5% salicylic (third-party listing). Newer formulas move toward gentler or "brightening" stacks: lactic + salicylic + tranexamic acid + niacinamide, alcohol-free and pH-balanced (Bushbalm). Older mass pads still use alcohol (Completely Bare: SD Alcohol 40 as the 2nd ingredient; Bliss: alcohol denat. as the 2nd ingredient). Counts cluster at 25–30, 42–55 and 60 pads.

## 3. Marketing angles, hooks and DTC tactics that work abroad

### Takeaway
Five proven angles: (1) **post-wax / post-shave bikini care** ("razor bumps", "bikini bumps", "bikini line confidence"); (2) **"strawberry legs" / KP / dark spots**, used to broaden the pads beyond the bikini line; (3) **men's razor bumps and grooming** (FAB for Men, Tend Skin, Anthony); (4) **"dermatologist tested", clean, vegan and compostable** credentials; (5) **taboo-busting, destigmatising pubic-care branding** (Fur, Bushbalm). The scale-up playbook was esthetician and wax-salon distribution + Amazon + Ulta/Target, plus TV pitch shows (Dragons' Den, Shark Tank).

### Inferences
- **Proven hooks to adapt for Bulgaria:** "no more bumps after waxing or shaving"; "smooth bikini line in 10 days" (FAB-style perception study); "strawberry legs"; a men's razor-bump version; "alcohol-free, won't sting".
- **Before/after limits.** EU claims must be substantiated under Reg. 655/2013 (see section 4). Before/after images must show real, unretouched results with a stated usage period. "Reduces the look of" phrasing (Bushbalm) transfers to the EU more safely than Fur's "heals ingrowns".
- **Bundle and subscription mechanics** (pads + oil or roller; 2-packs; travel 7-packs) are standard among US leaders and cheap to copy.
- **The salon / wax-studio B2B channel** was Bushbalm's biggest scale lever (moving toward about 75% pro channel by one account). It is likely replicable through Bulgarian waxing and laser studios, but that is an inference.

### Gaps
- I found no verifiable TikTok viral moment tied to a specific ingrown pad SKU with view or sales numbers.
- The claim that Bushbalm's pro channel could reach about 75% of the business comes from a search summary only (the BeautyHub article body was not retrieved).

## 4. EU regulatory constraints (salicylic acid, AHAs, CPNP, labelling, claims)

### Takeaway
Salicylic acid is **restricted under Annex III, entry 98** of Regulation (EC) 1223/2009, as amended by Reg. (EU) 2019/1966 from 1 Oct 2021. The limits are: 3.0% in rinse-off hair products; **2.0% in "other products"**; and **0.5% in body lotion**, eye shadow, mascara, eyeliner, lipstick and roll-on deodorant. It is banned in all products for children under 3, and labels must carry "Not to be used for children under 3 years of age". AHAs are not in the annexes, but the EU scientific committee recommends glycolic acid ≤4% at pH ≥3.8 (and lactic acid ≤2.5% at pH ≥5.0 per one secondary source), with sun-protection warnings. Every product needs an EU Responsible Person, a Product Information File (PIF) with safety assessment, and **CPNP notification** before sale. Bulgaria requires the Art. 19(1) label information **in Bulgarian**. Claims must stay cosmetic ("helps prevent", "reduces the look of", "exfoliates"); "treats folliculitis" or "heals" is medicinal territory.

### Cited Findings
- **Salicylic acid, Annex III entry 98:**
  - limits: rinse-off hair products 3.0%; other products 2.0%; body lotion, eye shadow, mascara, eyeliner, lipstick and roll-on deodorant 0.5%
  - "Not to be used in preparations for children under 3 years of age", with the label warning "Not to be used for children under 3 years of age"
  - not for applications that could expose the user's lungs by inhalation, and not for oral products
  - effective 1 Oct 2021 ([Coslaw.eu](https://coslaw.eu/omnibus-new-restrictions-for-salicylic-acid-and-titanium-dioxide-in-cosmetics/); [EUR-Lex, Reg. (EU) 2019/1966](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A32019R1966))
- Salicylic acid is also listed in Annex V (preservatives). The SCCS was mandated in January 2026 to assess aggregate exposure to salicylic acid, so limits could change ([ClearOrg](https://clearorg.eu/en/cosmetic-exfoliant-regulation-aha-bha-pha-eu-uk-usa/)).
- **AHAs.** Glycolic acid max 4% at pH ≥3.8 and lactic acid max 2.5% at pH ≥5.0 as recommendations. Precautions: "avoid exposure to the sun, use adequate sun protection, avoid contact with the eyes" ([ClearOrg](https://clearorg.eu/en/cosmetic-exfoliant-regulation-aha-bha-pha-eu-uk-usa/)). The 4% / pH 3.8 glycolic guidance comes from the former SCCNFP and is non-binding (search summary) ([SCCNFP/0370/00](https://ec.europa.eu/health/ph_risk/committees/sccp/documents/out121_en.pdf)). PHAs are not restricted in the EU, UK or US ([ClearOrg](https://clearorg.eu/en/cosmetic-exfoliant-regulation-aha-bha-pha-eu-uk-usa/)).
- **US contrast.** Salicylic acid with anti-acne claims is an OTC drug in the US. The FDA allows AHAs up to 10% at pH ≥3.5 with a "Sunburn Alert" label ([ClearOrg](https://clearorg.eu/en/cosmetic-exfoliant-regulation-aha-bha-pha-eu-uk-usa/)). US pad formulas at 4–5.9% glycolic (e.g., Bliss, per [WhatsInMyJar](https://whatsinmyjar.com/product/bliss-bump-attendant-pads)) would therefore exceed the EU's 4% recommendation.
- **Claims.** Reg. (EU) 655/2013 sets six common criteria for justifying cosmetic claims, applicable since 11 July 2013. Claims that a product prevents, treats or cures a medical condition can turn it into a medicinal product, and therapeutic claims implying action beyond the superficial layers of the skin should be avoided ([Ecomundo](https://www.ecomundo.eu/en/blog/eu-regulations-cosmetic-product-claims); [EC Technical document on cosmetic claims](https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/24847/attachments/1/translations/en/renditions/native); [ClearOrg borderline](https://clearorg.eu/en/borderline-cosmetic-products-regulation-europe/)).
- **CPNP and Responsible Person.** The CPNP is the EU's free online notification portal under 1223/2009, and notification must happen before market placement. A designated Responsible Person must ensure compliance, and a successful CPNP notification "does not necessarily mean" the product complies ([Cosmetics Institute EU](https://cosmeticsinstitute.eu/en/useful-information/)).
- **Bulgarian language.** Bulgarian Ordinance No. 14 of 28 July 2014 says pre-packaged cosmetics may be made available to consumers only with a durable, non-removable label, band, sticker or card **in Bulgarian** carrying the Art. 19(1) information (search summary of the State Gazette and Ministry of Health texts) ([Ordinance 14/2014 PDF](https://www.rzi-smolyan.com/norma/neredbi/NAREDBA___14_ot_28.07.2014.pdf); [Ministry of Health BG](https://www.mh.government.bg/bg/informaciya-za-grazhdani/kozmetichni-produkti/obshti-iziskvania)). Distributors must verify that the Art. 19(5) language requirements are met before making a product available (search summary) ([EUR-Lex 1223/2009](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2009/1223/oj/eng)).
- **Import cost for cross-border DTC.** From 1 Jul 2026 to Jul 2028 the EU applies a flat €3 customs duty per item type (by customs code) on parcels of €150 or less imported from outside the EU, ending duty-free treatment for low-value parcels. A €2 handling fee was also proposed ([VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/eu/eu-e3-levy-low-value-e-commerce-import-package-july-2026/); [EU Perspectives](https://euperspectives.eu/2025/12/eu-to-introduce-e3-duty-on-small-online-orders-from-july-2026/)).

### Inferences
- **Which salicylic limit applies to a leave-on body pad** (2% "other products" vs. 0.5% "body lotion") is an interpretation question for the safety assessor. The conservative route for an EU bikini pad is about 0.5% salicylic + ≤4% glycolic or lactic (pH ≥3.8), or up to 2% BHA only with a documented "not a body lotion" rationale. US formulas above these levels (e.g., Bliss) cannot simply be imported unchanged.
- **Mandatory label elements for Bulgaria:** "Not to be used for children under 3 years of age" (salicylic), sun-protection advice (AHA), and full Bulgarian labelling (sticker acceptable).
- **Claim wording.** Safe: "helps prevent ingrown hairs", "exfoliates and smooths", "reduces the look of razor bumps and redness after hair removal", "for the bikini line". Avoid: "treats folliculitis / pseudofolliculitis", "heals ingrowns", "anti-inflammatory treatment", "cures razor bumps".
- Importing US brands' pads via cross-border DTC now costs about €3 duty plus VAT per order. That makes local EU-stocked supply more competitive in Bulgaria.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the exact Annex V preservative limit for salicylic acid or any Commission/SCCS guidance classifying "toner pads" under the 0.5% vs. 2% rows.
- Mandelic acid: no EU-specific guidance found.
- No source found on Bulgarian enforcement of ingrown-hair claims specifically (e.g., KZP or Ministry of Health actions).

## 5. Manufacturing and sourcing (OEM / private label)
