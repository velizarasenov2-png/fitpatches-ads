## 1. Who removes pubic/bikini hair, how, and who gets ingrown hairs (by gender, age, method)

### Takeaway
No Bulgarian survey on pubic hair removal or ingrown hairs was found. Proxies show this is mostly a women's habit: about 70–85% of women in the US and Western Europe remove some pubic hair, compared with about 40–55% of men in the EU. Removal is most common among women under about 45, and most of it is done at home with a blade razor (about 67–90% of women). Ingrown hairs are among the two most common complications, reported by about one-third of women who remove pubic hair in a US clinic sample. Shaving, especially close shaving with a blade, is the main cause. Waxing and plucking cause ingrown hairs too, and laser is the clinical fix.

### Cited Findings
**Prevalence, women (proxies)**
- US, nationally representative (GfK panel, January 2013, n=3,316 women aged 18–65): **83.8% reported pubic hair grooming**. Compared with women aged 18–24, women aged 45–55 (OR 0.05) and over 55 (OR 0.04) were much less likely to groom. Some college (OR 3.36) or a bachelor's degree (OR 2.39) raised the odds. Grooming was **not associated with income, relationship status or geography**. — [Rowen et al., JAMA Dermatology 2016 (PubMed 27367465)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27367465/)
- US, probability survey (n=7,580, aged 18–65): 74% groom their pubic hair, **84% of women and 66% of men**. — [Osterberg et al., Sexually Transmitted Infections 2017 (PubMed 27920223)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27920223/)
- **EU proxy, Belgium (Flanders), online survey of 1,735 women and 2,687 men: 80.3% of women vs 39.1% of men (partially) remove pubic hair.** The practice was associated with age, sexual activity, relationship status and the partner's preference. Only women cited "feeling feminine" as a reason. — [Enzlin et al., J Sex Med 2019 (PubMed 31103484)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31103484/)
- **EU proxy, Germany, national random sample (September–October 2016, n=2,510, aged 14–94): 69% of women vs 41% of men remove body hair.** Removal was associated with **younger age, higher education, higher income and living in an urban region**. — [Body hair removal in Germany, J Cosmet Dermatol 2020 (PubMed 32167230)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32167230/)
- **EU proxy, Galaxus multi-country survey (search excerpt only, page returned HTTP 503): in Austria 74% of women and 55% of men trim or shave their pubic hair; in France 58% of women and 40% of men.** 82% of women and 48% of men remove underarm hair. — [Galaxus survey](https://www.galaxus.at/en/page/galaxus-survey-which-european-country-is-most-bothered-by-body-hair-33134)
- Meta-analysis of 22 studies (73,091 participants, multiple countries, women): pooled grooming prevalence 93.54%, complete removal 50.3%, partial 33.1%. **Non-electric razor is the most common method (69.3%).** Pooled side effects: genital itching 26.9%, rash 10.2%, **folliculitis 7.2%**, allergy 2%. — [Systematic review and meta-analysis, BMC Women's Health 2024 (PMC10926629)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10926629/)

**Methods by gender (US proxy)**
- US nationally representative (JAMA Dermatology 2017): **85.3% of women and 66.5% of men ever groomed.** Main methods for women: **non-electric razor 66.7%**, electric razor 13.3%, scissors 12.0%, **wax 4.9%**, laser/electrolysis 0.5%. For men: non-electric razor 47.5%, **electric razor 26.9%**, scissors 18.4%, wax 0.5%, laser 0.6%. — [Gaither et al., JAMA Dermatology 2017 (PMC5710443)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5710443/)
- College sample (n=1,110): 95% had removed pubic hair in the past 4 weeks. **Shaving was the most common method for women (82%) and men (49%).** 50% of women vs 19% of men said their typical state was hair-free. **80.3% of groomers had genital itching at least once.** — [Herbenick et al., J Sex Med 2015 (PubMed 25394526)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25394526/)
- German dermatology society review (61 publications): **depilation methods (shaving, trimming, chemical creams) are used more often than epilation methods** (waxing, sugaring, epilator, laser, IPL). — [JDDG 2023 (PubMed 36960835)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36960835/)

**Ingrown hairs and complications**
- **US women aged 16–40 at public clinics (n=333, 2012): 32.7% reported ingrown hairs and 36.7% epidermal abrasion (the two most common complications); 59.5% had at least one complication.** Other complications: severe itching 21.1%, cuts 18.4%, rash 13.2%, infection 4.9%. **Razor blade was used by 89.5%**, depilatory cream by 16.0%, wax by 7.2%. 62.2% removed all their pubic hair. Overweight or obese women were about twice as likely to report a complication, and about three times as likely if they also removed all their hair. **Only 3.9% sought medical care.** — [DeMaria et al., AJOG 2014 (PMC4040320)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4040320/)
- **Grooming injuries (US 2017): 25.6% of groomers were injured (women 27.1%, men 23.7%).** Lacerations made up 61.2%, burns 23.0% and rashes 12.2% of injuries. High-frequency groomers (11 or more full removals) had higher injury odds: 1.97× for men and 2.21× for women. **Waxing was protective against high-frequency injuries in women (aOR 0.11).** Injured groomers were younger (women 36.4 vs 42.5 years). The study did not track ingrown hairs. — [Gaither et al., JAMA Dermatology 2017 (PMC5710443)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5710443/)
- Contrast: Saudi women (n=400, all remove pubic hair, mean age 26.3): 75.5% had any complication but **only 8.9% reported ingrown hairs**. Methods there were razor 33.5%, laser 8.7%, sugaring 6.0% and wax 4.5%. Ingrown-hair prevalence varies widely between samples. — [Saudi study, BMC Women's Health 2018 (PMC6196448)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6196448/)

**Which method causes the most ingrown hairs**
- Pseudofolliculitis ("razor bumps") results from **close shaving** that leaves sharp hair tips. It is "more common with blade razor users compared to electric shavers". It can occur anywhere hair is shaved or plucked, including the pubic area and legs. It mainly affects men with tightly curled hair (about 45–80% of men of African ancestry) but also affects women of all races, "especially occurring in the groin". Prevention listed: shave with the grain, switch to electric shavers, **glycolic-acid moisturisers**, chemical depilatories and laser epilation. — [DermNet: Pseudofolliculitis barbae](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae)
- Folliculitis can arise as hairs regrow after shaving, waxing, electrolysis or plucking. — [DermNet: Folliculitis](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/folliculitis)
- Causes of ingrown hair: shaving, tweezing, waxing ("Removing hair makes it more likely to grow back into the skin"). — [healthdirect (Australian government)](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/ingrown-hair)
- Laser hair removal is "effective at treating PFB" (US military cohort treated with 4–6 sessions). — [Military Medicine 2026 (PubMed 41500510)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41500510/)
- Post-waxing folliculitis is documented as a distinct clinicopathological entity. — [Int J Dermatol (PubMed 24134338)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24134338/)

### Inferences
- Ranked from the cited sources, a qualitative ranking of ingrown-hair risk is: **blade razor (close shave) > waxing/sugaring/epilator (plucking methods) > electric trimmer > laser** (laser is the treatment, not a cause). No study gives head-to-head ingrown-hair rates by method for pubic hair. The ranking is inferred from mechanism (DermNet) and from the fact that ingrown hairs top the complications list in the razor-dominated AJOG sample.
- The core sufferer is a **woman aged about 18–44 who shaves her bikini or pubic area at home with a blade razor, often removing all of it**. She has probably never seen a doctor about it (about 96% do not). Proxies point to younger, more educated and urban women, which overlaps with Bulgaria's online-buyer profile in section 4. Because grooming showed no income association in the US, the problem is not income-limited, but the ability to pay for laser is.
- Applying proxy rates to Bulgaria is uncertain. Bulgarian forum voice (background report) confirms shaving, waxing and epilators are all used and that ingrown hairs in the bikini line ("подкожни косми") are a recognised, shame-laden problem. There is no number.

### Gaps
- **No Bulgarian (or Eastern European) survey** on pubic hair removal prevalence, method mix or ingrown hairs was found (searches in Bulgarian for проучване/обезкосмяване/бикини/Notino/Тренд/Галъп returned only clinic marketing). Dias.bg claims "32.7% of people aged 19–34 prefer laser", but gives no source, so it is excluded ([Dias.bg](https://dias.bg/lazerna-epilacia-gentle-lase/)).
- No study reports ingrown-hair prevalence **by method** in the pubic area, or by skin/hair type among Europeans.
- The full text of JDDG 2023 (method-specific risk tables) was blocked (HTTP 403).

## 2. Bulgarian hair-removal market: laser and wax prices, home vs salon, Bulgarian surveys

### Takeaway
Hair removal in Bulgaria is a crowded, low-priced, promotion-driven salon market. A single laser bikini session costs about €13–40, full intimate about €21–50, and 6-session packages about €150–250. Salon waxing of the intimate area costs about €10–15. A full laser course for the intimate area therefore costs roughly €150–300+, compared with a €20 pad. No data was found on how many women shave at home versus wax at a salon versus use laser, or on how many clinics exist.

### Inferences
- **Price anchors for a €20 pad:** about one laser bikini session, or roughly 1.5–2 salon intimate waxes. Against a full laser course (about €150–300 for 6 sessions, often more for full intimate), a €20 pad is about a tenth of the cost. That supports the angle "домашната стъпка преди/вместо лазера" (the at-home step before or instead of laser).
- Laser promotions are aggressive (€13–25 entry prices), and clinics actively market laser as the cure for ingrown hairs. Women on a laser course still shave between sessions, so they still get ingrown hairs. This is a plausible secondary segment (inference).
- Salon wax is cheap (€10–15), so waxers are a sizeable group, but there is no Bulgarian data to size them.

## 3. Men: is the male segment real for intimate ingrown-hair products?

### Takeaway
It is real but secondary. Proxies show fewer men remove pubic hair (about 39–55% in EU proxies vs about 74–80% of women), men use electric trimmers more (lower ingrown risk), and **Bulgarian men buy cosmetics online at about a quarter of the women's rate (4.0% vs 17.6% in a 3-month window, 2024)**. The male segment is better treated as a later expansion (body/neck razor bumps, "manscaping") than as the core ICP.

### Cited Findings
- **Bulgaria, Eurostat 2024 (last 3 months, % of individuals 16–74): cosmetics, beauty or wellness products bought online by 4.0% of men vs 17.59% of women.** Men 25–34: 6.83%; 35–44: 6.75%; 45–54: 3.77%. EU-27 men: 11.08%. Personal hygiene/cleaning products online: men 1.86% vs women 5.61%. — [Eurostat isoc_ec_ibgs](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/isoc_ec_ibgs/default/table?lang=en) ([API query](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/isoc_ec_ibgs?geo=BG&geo=EU27_2020&time=2024&unit=PC_IND&format=JSON&lang=EN))
- EU proxies: Belgium (Flanders), 39.1% of men vs 80.3% of women remove pubic hair ([J Sex Med 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31103484/)). Germany, 41% of men vs 69% of women remove body hair ([J Cosmet Dermatol 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32167230/)). Austria, 55% of men; France, 40% of men trim/shave pubic hair ([Galaxus excerpt](https://www.galaxus.at/en/page/galaxus-survey-which-european-country-is-most-bothered-by-body-hair-33134)).
- UK: "Nearly half of all British men regularly shave their bodies… 57% of 16- to 24-year-old males trim their pubic hair" (Guardian 2024, from search excerpt; page blocked to fetch). — [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/article/2024/aug/29/why-are-so-many-men-now-manscaping)
- US men (n=4,198): **50.5% groom pubic hair regularly**, declining with age (OR 0.95 per year). Top motivations: sex (peak 73% at 25–34), hygiene 61%. — [Gaither et al., Am J Men's Health 2016 (PMC5675231)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5675231/)
- US men, Ipsos KnowledgePanel April 2023: **46.7% lifetime pubic hair removal, 29.2% in the past 30 days.** Gay and bisexual men 76.5% vs heterosexual men 44.6%. Being sexually active predicted removal. — [Am J Men's Health 2024 (PubMed 39340386)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39340386/)
- Men's methods (US 2017): non-electric razor 47.5%, electric razor 26.9%, scissors 18.4%. Men's injury rate 23.7% vs women 27.1%. — [JAMA Dermatology 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5710443/). Men who have sex with men report more grooming infections (7.0% vs 1.0%) and abscesses (8.8% vs 2.5%). — [J Sex Med 2015 (PubMed 25442701)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25442701/)
- PFB is more common with blade razors than electric shavers, and mainly affects men with curly, coarse hair. — [DermNet](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/pseudofolliculitis-barbae)
- dm Bulgaria content: "on average a man shaves 16,000 times in his life… many men suffer from skin…" (problems; snippet). — [dm България](https://www.dm-drogeriemarkt.bg/vdahnovenie-i-saveti/mujki-svjat/grizha-za-litse-za-mazha-2176648)
- Background report: the only male intimate-grooming ad shown in Bulgaria was in English (BOTANE MAN), and a LUMI review comes from a man with neck razor bumps.

### Inferences
- Rough relative size: Bulgarian men buy cosmetics online at about 23% of women's rate (4.0/17.6), and remove pubic hair at about half of women's rate (EU proxies). So the reachable male intimate-pad buyer pool is plausibly about 10–25% of the female pool. This is a back-of-envelope inference from proxies.
- The stronger male hook is probably beard/neck "razor bumps" plus body grooming, rather than the intimate area alone. Male creatives should be tested only after the female core works.

### Gaps
- No Bulgarian data on male grooming, "manscaping", or the size of the men's shaving/grooming market (searches found only retailer pages).
- Galaxus/Guardian figures are from search excerpts; the full pages could not be fetched.

## 4. Bulgarian e-commerce buyers: who buys online, who buys cosmetics online, COD vs card, age/gender, region, income

### Takeaway
Only about half of Bulgarians aged 16–74 buy online (51.2% in the last 12 months, 2025; EU 73.6%). Women buy more than men, and **women aged 16–44 are by far the most active buyers (70–75% buy online yearly; about 29% bought cosmetics online in a 3-month window in 2024)**. Buying is concentrated in cities and the South-West (Sofia) region. **Cash on delivery remains the largest payment method (50.3% of e-commerce payments in 2024; 92.9% of stores offer it).** The average gross wage is about €1,444 per month (Q2 2026), and €1,915 in Sofia. A €20 product is about 1.4% of the average wage but 3.2% of the €620 minimum wage, so price sensitivity outside Sofia is real.

### Inferences
- **Highest-propensity buyer:** a woman aged 25–44 in Sofia or another large city (Varna, Plovdiv), employed, smartphone-first. About 29% of women 25–44 bought cosmetics online in a 3-month window (2024), about 3× the national average and about 7× men. Women 45–54 are a meaningful second tier (18%).
- **Rough funnel (inference, proxies):** Bulgaria has about 1.65 M people aged 25–44 ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bulgaria): 10.3% + 14.3% of 6.70 M), so roughly 0.8 M women aged 25–44. If about 29% buy cosmetics online per quarter (Eurostat), that is about 235k. If about 80% remove pubic hair (Belgian/US proxies), about 190k. If about one-third get ingrown hairs (AJOG proxy), **on the order of 60–70k women aged 25–44 are in-market**, plus younger 18–24 and older 45–54 tiers. Treat this as an order of magnitude only.
- **COD is mandatory for conversion.** Half of payments are COD and 93% of stores offer it. A single-product Meta store without COD would likely lose a large share of buyers, especially outside Sofia and among less digitally skilled buyers (only 38% have basic digital skills). Expect unclaimed-parcel losses (the 5–20% claim is unverified). Trust signals and a confirmation call or SMS matter.
- **Price:** €20 is about 1.4% of the average gross wage (1.0% in Sofia, 3.2% at minimum wage). It fits impulse buying for urban employed women but is a considered purchase in low-wage regions (South-East online buying is only 37.7%). Two-pack bundles (about €40) are realistic mainly in Sofia, Varna and Plovdiv (inference).

### Gaps
- No Bulgarian breakdown of COD share **by category** (cosmetics) or by age/region, and no eMAG, Econt or Speedy public data on COD share for 2025–2026 (searches returned nothing usable).
- Eurostat 2025 cosmetics-category data is not yet published (2024 is the latest).
- Bulgarian cosmetics retailer reports (Notino, dm, eMAG, Lilly) with category sizes or customer demographics were not found.

## 5. Social media audiences in Bulgaria (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok) and reachable women 18–44

### Takeaway
Meta reaches the whole target. Facebook has 3.6–4.5 M users in Bulgaria (51–52% female), with about 1.17 M women aged 18–44 (NapoleonCat, September 2026). Instagram has about 2.1 M users (about 54% female), with about 0.77 M women aged 18–44. **TikTok's adult ad audience is 2.63 M and 61.4% female, about 1.6 M women 18+, and grew +21% y/y.** It is the most female-skewed platform and the fastest-growing reach channel. Meta's age/gender counts exceed census figures, so treat them as upper bounds.

### Cited Findings
- **DataReportal Digital 2026 Bulgaria (data as of October/late 2025):** internet users 5.84 M (87.1%). Social media user identities 4.50 M (67.1%); 3.98 M aged 18+ (72.0% of adults); 49.7% female. — [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bulgaria)
  - **Facebook: 3.60 M ad reach; 65.1% of adults 18+; adult audience 51.4% female / 48.6% male.** Reach was −2.7% y/y but +5.9% quarter on quarter.
  - **Instagram: 1.95 M; 35.3% of adults; 53.0% female / 46.2% male;** +5.4% y/y.
  - **TikTok: 2.63 M users aged 18+; 47.6% of adults; 61.4% female / 38.6% male; +463k (+21.4%) y/y.**
  - YouTube: 4.50 M ad reach; 49.7% female.
- **NapoleonCat, Facebook Bulgaria, September 2026: 4,460,700 users (67.1% of population); 51.9% women.** Shares of the total audience by age (women / men): 18–24 5.8% / 5.5%; **25–34 10.5% / 11.2% (largest group, 970,500)**; 35–44 9.9% / 10.4%; 45–54 9.6% / 9.4%; 55–64 7.8% / 6.5%; 65+ 8.3% / 5.1%. Monthly range October 2025–September 2026: **4.32 M (April 2026) to 4.73 M (August 2026)**, with peaks in June (4.71 M) and August. — [NapoleonCat Facebook BG](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/facebook-users-in-bulgaria/)
- **NapoleonCat, Instagram Bulgaria, September 2026: 2,115,400 users (31.8%); 53.7% women.** Age shares (women / men): 18–24 8.8% / 8.4%; **25–34 15.4% / 15.7% (largest, 657,400)**; 35–44 12.0% / 10.3%; 45–54 9.4% / 6.8%; 55–64 4.9% / 3.2%; 65+ 3.3% / 1.9%. The monthly peak was June 2026 (2.35 M). — [NapoleonCat Instagram BG](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/instagram-users-in-bulgaria/)
- NapoleonCat's text claims "women lead by 229,100 (65+)" on Facebook and "143,400 (45–54)" on Instagram. These do not match its own percentage tables (they equal the men's shares × total), so they look like a site bug and are not used.

### Inferences
- **Derived reachable audiences (own calculation from NapoleonCat September 2026 shares × totals):**
  - Facebook women 18–44 ≈ **1.17 M**; women 25–44 ≈ 0.91 M; women 18–54 ≈ 1.60 M; men 18–44 ≈ 1.21 M.
  - Instagram women 18–44 ≈ **0.77 M**; women 25–44 ≈ 0.58 M; women 18–54 ≈ 0.96 M; men 18–44 ≈ 0.73 M.
  - TikTok women 18+ ≈ **1.61 M**; men 18+ ≈ 1.02 M (DataReportal late-2025 shares).
- **Meta inflates audiences.** UN population data imply about 2.10 M people aged 18–44 in Bulgaria (31.3% of 6.70 M), so about 1.0 M women. NapoleonCat's 1.17 M Facebook women 18–44 exceeds that, likely because of duplicate accounts, the diaspora and tourists (the summer peaks coincide with the tourist season). DataReportal's lower Facebook figure (3.60 M) reflects different source timing and methodology. Practical takeaway: Meta can reach close to every Bulgarian woman aged 18–44, and the limit is creative and budget, not audience size.
- **Platform fit:** Instagram over-indexes on women 18–44 (36% of its audience vs 26% on Facebook), which matches the cosmetics-buyer profile. Facebook adds women 45–54 and the regions. TikTok is 61% female and growing fastest, which makes it the most efficient awareness channel for a shame-laden "explain the problem" product (inference). Meta remains the conversion channel given COD/Shopify setups.

### Gaps
- No TikTok age split for Bulgaria (only 18+ total and gender).
- No Bulgarian data on interest-audience sizes (e.g., "laser hair removal", "waxing" interests in Meta Ads Manager), because the Meta Ads connector was not used per the constraints.

## 6. Seasonality: bikini/beach season and when ingrown-hair demand peaks

### Takeaway
Bulgarian leisure travel peaks sharply in July–August, and June–September accounts for about 45% of Bulgarians' annual hotel registrations. This is the "visible bikini zone" window. Laser clinics push course starts in autumn, and DTC competitors advertised from May through October. Search-volume seasonality for "врастнали косми" could not be measured because Google Trends returned HTTP 429.

### Cited Findings
- **Bulgarian tourists' accommodation registrations by month, 2025:** January 318,162; May 408,073; **June 503,265; July 667,774; August 734,619 (peak); September 500,071**; October 385,470; December 390,949; total 5,363,922 (+2.9% y/y). Bulgarians' nights in 2025: 12.73 M. — [Ministry of Tourism, 2025 statistics based on NSI (PDF)](https://www.tourism.government.bg/sites/tourism.government.bg/files/documents/2026-03/statisticheski_danni_tourism_2025.pdf)
- NSI Q3 2025: **1.9 M Bulgarians made tourist trips**; 74.2% travelled only domestically and 21.3% only abroad. **"Почивка и екскурзия" (holiday) was the purpose of 65.5% of domestic and 84.5% of foreign trips.** People aged 45–64 were 39.2% of travellers, and 25–44-year-olds had the highest share travelling abroad (23.3%). — [Bgtourism.bg on NSI, 21 November 2025](https://bgtourism.bg/nsi-1-9-miliona-bulgari-turisticheski-patuvania-treto-trimesechie/)
- Bulgarians' trips abroad in June 2026: 986.8k (+4.6% y/y). — [NSI June 2026](https://www.nsi.bg/press-release/patuvaniya-na-balgarski-grajdani-v-chujbina-i-poseshteniya-na-chujdi-grajdani-v-balgariya-yuni-2026-godina-predvaritelni-danni-9086)
- Meta audience size also peaks in summer: Facebook BG reached 4.71 M in June and 4.73 M in August 2026 vs 4.32 M in April; Instagram peaked at 2.35 M in June 2026. — [NapoleonCat FB](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/facebook-users-in-bulgaria/); [NapoleonCat IG](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/instagram-users-in-bulgaria/)
- Laser marketing frames autumn as course-start season ("Есента е предпочитан период за стартиране на процедурите"). — [studio24.bg Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/studio24.bg/)
- Background report: Deroli launched ads on 4 May, 20 June and in August, and refreshed creatives on 1 October 2026. LUMI launched in late September 2026. Clinics run "Не отлагайте гладката кожа за следващото лято" (don't put off smooth skin until next summer).
- Forum voice (background report) ties the pain to summer: "По цяло лято ходех с дълъг панталон…", "не ми е комфортно през летните месеци да ходя с къси панталонки".

### Inferences
- **Demand calendar (inference):** pre-season ramp in May–June (first beach/pool exposure, peak shaving frequency), peak in July–August (sea holidays, about 1.4M+ Bulgarian hotel registrations in those two months), and a September tail. Ingrown-hair complaints should lag peak shaving by days to weeks, so the late-June to August window is likely the conversion peak.
- **Off-season angles:** October to March are not dead. Laser-course starters still shave between sessions, intimacy/hygiene motivations are year-round, and DTC competitors are advertising in October 2026. Expect lower conversion than in summer but cheaper CPMs (inference, unmeasured).
- 45–64-year-olds dominate domestic travel volume, but the core buyer is 18–44 (sections 1, 4, 5). Beach season is a trigger for the young core, not a reason to target older travellers.

### Gaps
- **Google Trends for Bulgaria was blocked (HTTP 429 on API and UI)**, so there is no measured search seasonality for "врастнали косми", "враснали косми" or "подкожни косми". No published study of ingrown-hair search seasonality was found (only hair-loss seasonality studies exist).
- No official source was retrieved for the exact Black Sea season dates. The June–September window is inferred from monthly tourism registrations, not from a season-definition source.
- Monthly breakdown of Bulgarians' trips by destination (Black Sea coast vs other) was not retrieved.
