## Q1. Patch brands: did they make Halloween-shaped patches or limited packaging, and how did the website look?

### Takeaway
The big pimple-patch DTC brands — Starface, Hero/Mighty Patch, ZitSticka, Peace Out and Rael — have **no traceable Halloween-shaped patches and no Halloween site takeover**. Their seasonal effort goes into **Christmas/holiday limited patches**, which go live in early October, plus colourway or licensing drops such as Starface's Black Star in Oct 2022 and Hello Kitty in Oct 2026. The patch brand that *does* own Halloween is the smaller **SpaLife / My Spa Life**. It runs a dedicated, cute-spooky line of shaped hydrocolloid patches (ghosts, skulls, pumpkins, bats, cats, witch hats, glow-in-the-dark and holographic), including a **pink "Pink-O-Ween" pack**, and gives it a full homepage takeover. Wellness/vitamin patch brands (Kind Patches, The Good Patch, PatchAid) do nothing for Halloween.

### Inferences
- A Halloween patch line is a **niche, cute-gift play**: SpaLife positions it as "boo basket stuffers" under $20. The large acne-patch brands skip Halloween and start holiday in early October. For a wellness or weight-loss patch, the realistic copyable pattern is **SpaLife's site treatment, not new patch shapes**: an emoji nav label, a second promo bar, a pun headline, and badges on cards.
- **Pink-O-Ween is the most relevant single reference for fitpatches.** It proves that "Halloween" can be done entirely in pink and pastel, with small ghost, skull and pumpkin icons, and no black or orange.
- No wellness or vitamin patch brand was found doing Halloween. Inference: fitpatches would be first in its niche, but it also has no peer proof that Halloween lifts weight-loss patch sales.

### Gaps
- **Not verified:** what Starface, Hero, ZitSticka, Peace Out and Kind Patches homepages looked like on 20–31 October of 2021–2025. Wayback is blocked from this environment and Firecrawl credits are exhausted; see the URL list in Q4.
- PatchMD could not be checked (HTTP 403).
- products.json only shows *currently published* products. Halloween SKUs that were unpublished after past seasons would not appear, so "no Halloween SKU" means "none currently published", not "never existed".
- The claim that Starface's iridescent "Cyber" stars sold out in 72 hours appeared only in a search summary and was not verified, so it is excluded.

## Q2. Beauty brands with well-known Halloween drops: colour palettes, typography, hero imagery, animation, cursor/favicon, spooky product names

### Takeaway
The best-documented "Halloween" moments in mass beauty come from **licensed horror or witch IP drops** with themed packaging: ColourPop x Hocus Pocus (2020–2022), ColourPop x Nightmare Before Christmas (2021), Kylie x A Nightmare on Elm Street (2021), Kylie x Batman (2022), and wet n wild's annual Fantasy Makers line. These were promoted as **hero banners and collection pages, not full site re-skins**. On 2026-10-25 the observed pattern was:
- **Full hero takeover** with an IP-style dark or neon look: wet n wild (cosmic black, neon green, purple).
- **Light touch**: ColourPop (Halloween only in the announcement bar, "👻" emoji, a "Spooky Mystery Box" tile) and Jeffree Star (a "Spooky Bags" carousel slide plus Halloween merch).
- **No Halloween at all and already on Christmas**: Kylie, Fenty, e.l.f., Lush, Glossier, MAC.

No favicon swaps were seen. The only motion seen was seasonal *holiday* motion: Kylie's falling-snowflake overlay, plus marquee tickers at Hero and wet n wild.

## Q3. Which approach dominated: full dark-mode takeover, light touch (bar + hero + badges), or "cute/cosy autumn"? Who kept their brand colour?

### Takeaway
**Light touch, brand colour kept** is the dominant pattern. In a cross-section of 17 beauty, skincare and patch homepages captured on 2026-10-25:
- Only **2 ran a full Halloween hero takeover**: SpaLife (cute pastel lavender and purple, "Boo for your skin") and wet n wild (dark cosmic, neon green and purple).
- **2 used light-touch accents**: ColourPop (announcement bar + 👻 + mystery-box tile) and Jeffree Star (one carousel slide + merch).
- **7 had already switched to Christmas or holiday**: Hero, Kylie, Fenty, e.l.f., Lush, Glossier, MAC; ColourPop's hero also counts here.

None of them recoloured the whole site to black and orange. Every brand kept its logo, header and brand colours (ColourPop's pink bar, SpaLife's magenta and purple, Starface's yellow, black and neon). The "cute-spooky / Pinkoween" style (pastel pink and lavender with ghosts, pumpkins, bows) is the documented trend that matches a women-focused pink brand.

## Q4. How long before 31 Oct did brands switch site visuals, and when did they switch back?

### Takeaway
Halloween **product** launches go live very early: **July–August** for Bath & Body Works and the patch specialist SpaLife, and **late September to mid October** for beauty IP drops (ColourPop Sept 22 – Oct 1; Kylie Oct 12; Fenty Oct 13, 2017; Starface Black Star Oct 12, 2022). Many DTC brands then **move the homepage to holiday or Christmas in early-to-mid October**, before Halloween. Hero's holiday patches were published Oct 2 and Oct 6, 2026, and by Oct 25, 2026 Hero, Kylie, Fenty, e.l.f., Lush, Glossier and MAC were all on holiday. Halloween is often left only in the announcement bar (ColourPop) or a carousel slide (Jeffree Star). The "switch back" is effectively a switch **forward** to holiday or Black Friday. The exact Nov 1 change-over could not be observed.

## Q5. Any measured results (sales lift, engagement, sell-outs) attributed to these seasonal visuals?

### Takeaway
No source attributes a measured lift to **website visuals** specifically. The evidence that exists is about **limited Halloween or spooky products and IP drops**: sell-outs in minutes or hours (ColourPop Hocus Pocus 2020 and 2021; e.l.f. x Liquid Death Corpse Paint in 45 minutes), site traffic (+50% at Milani after its 2020 Halloween launch) and new-customer acquisition (68% new-to-brand for the e.l.f. x Liquid Death collection). The data supports "scarcity + themed product + social" more than "themed homepage".

### Cited Findings
- **ColourPop Hocus Pocus 2020:** the full $130 collection set sold out, after a launch delayed by "site complications" (inference: a traffic surge). [Disney Food Blog](https://www.disneyfoodblog.com/2020/10/01/hurry-the-new-hocus-pocus-colourpop-collection-is-selling-out-quickly/)

### Inferences
- What drives results in this category is a **limited, named, giftable item plus urgency** ("won't last past Halloween", "limited edition"), not a site re-skin. For fitpatches the analogue would be a time-limited "Halloween bundle" or a small themed gift, sold under light-touch site visuals in brand pink.
- Expected impact cannot be quantified from these sources. Any lift should be measured with an A/B or before/after comparison of hero CTR and conversion over Oct 15–31.

### Gaps
- No public A/B test or case study isolates the effect of Halloween *homepage visuals* for any beauty or patch brand.
- No sales figures were found for SpaLife's Halloween patch line. Its product reviews are sparse (0–1 reviews on most Halloween SKUs, observed 2026-10-25), which hints at modest DTC volume, but this is an inference.
- No Bulgarian or EU market data on Halloween beauty sales was found; outside scope.
