# Anatomy, Formats and Performance Benchmarks of High-Converting Advertorials (Pre-sell / Pre-lander Pages) for Health and Weight-Loss DTC Offers

Research date: 3 October 2026. Context: FitPatches (berberine transdermal patches, Bulgaria), Meta ads → advertorial → offer page, cash on delivery (COD).

How to read these notes:
- "HARD DATA" means a controlled study or measured dataset with a stated method.
- "MARKETER CLAIM" means numbers or opinions from an agency, vendor, tool or practitioner with no published method. Most numbers in this field are marketer claims.
- "(snippet)" means the claim was seen only in a search-result summary and the page itself was not fetched.
- "HISTORICAL" means the source is from before 2022.

## 1. Advertorial formats, and which win for cold vs warm traffic in health and weight loss

### Takeaway
Practitioners match the format to the reader's awareness stage, which follows Schwartz's awareness levels. Long story or education advertorials are used for cold, unaware or problem-aware traffic. Listicles ("X reasons why") are used for problem-aware or solution-aware traffic and warmer audiences. PDP or "trojan-horse" product pages are used for product-aware traffic. Quizzes and VSLs are niche choices. No public controlled A/B test settles advertorial vs listicle, so the "advertorial for cold, listicle for warm" rule is practitioner consensus, not proven fact. In 2025–2026 shorter, "compressed" advertorials are reported to win more often than they used to.

### Cited Findings
**Format taxonomies**
- LanderLab names 5 presell formats and when each works:
  - **Advertorial**: native and Meta traffic for health, finance and home services. Headline matches the ad, a protagonist story, social proof, a natural transition to the offer, a clear CTA.
  - **Quiz funnel**: 4–8 questions, a progress bar, a results page and conditional routing. Best for insurance, solar, debt and high-ticket offers.
  - **VSL pre-lander**: offers over $500, complex products, older demographics. 8–15 minute video, CTA shown after the video.
  - **Listicle/review**: byline, date, pros and cons, comparison table, "editor's pick" badge.
  - **Problem/solution**: search or retargeting traffic.
  - LanderLab gives no benchmarks per format. — [LanderLab, 2026](https://landerlab.io/blog/presell-page-examples)
- Trent Turner (DTC CRO, Mar 2024) lists 5 page types worth testing:
  1. **Trojan horse page**: a simplified PDP-like page with a bold hero, reviews, a "why" section, a brag bar, how it works and a comparison chart. For Meta prospecting and non-brand search.
  2. **Listicle** ("5 reasons why…"): for fast-scrolling upper-funnel traffic (TikTok, YouTube).
  3. **Advertorial**: for Facebook prospecting, often run from third-party accounts.
  4. **Quiz funnel**.
  5. **Social-proof/UGC page**: for fence-sitters. — [Trent Turner Substack, 2024](https://trentturner.substack.com/p/5-landing-pages-that-are-worth-testing)
- Trent Turner says advertorial *ads* get "higher click-through-rates… because they don't look like a typical branded ad". Running them from a third-party (publisher-style) page also lets brands test edgier, unbranded angles with less brand risk. MARKETER CLAIM. — [Trent Turner, 2024](https://trentturner.substack.com/p/5-landing-pages-that-are-worth-testing)
- HISTORICAL (Aug 2016), native networks: Adbeat lists 7 landing-page styles on Taboola:
  - blogvertorial
  - classic advertorial (news-article style on a separate domain)
  - classic pre-sell page before a longer sales letter
  - VSL
  - sponsored third-party blogvertorial (e.g., Business Insider)
  - action/quiz opt-in
  - affiliate "Top 10" review page
  - Health and supplement brands (Nugenix, ForceFactor) used pre-sell and VSL pages. HelloFresh spent about $599.2K on Taboola over 6 months, mostly sending traffic to blogvertorials. — [Adbeat, 2016](https://blog.adbeat.com/7-landing-page-styles-that-convert-on-native-part-1-taboola/)
- Apexure reviews 9 examples, all MARKETER-reported:
  - Article format: DOOR3, Reggie, Javy Coffee, Crown & Paw
  - 5-point listicle: Earthling Co., Jones Road
  - State-selector to multi-step form: Wealth Hub
  - Short-form: DeFolio
  - Competitor comparison page: Motion
  - Apexure suggests about 800 words for consumer products and 1,500–2,000 for B2B. — [Apexure, updated Mar 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/advertorials-a-detailed-guide-to-design-and-best-practices)

**Matching format to awareness stage**
- AdLibrary's mapping:
  - **Advertorial** → unaware or problem-aware readers, when the ad introduced a problem framing or mechanism the market doesn't yet accept.
  - **Editorial listicle** → problem-aware or solution-aware readers who need a fast case for the solution category.
  - **Product page (hybrid or straight)** → solution-aware or product-aware readers, when the ad already did the convincing. — [AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/advertorial-landing-pages)
- Landra (an AI advertorial/listicle vendor, Jun 2026) says outright that "there is no public controlled A/B test that pits the two formats against matched traffic". Its other points:
  - "Advertorials win cold, listicles win warm" is "near-universal consensus among DTC media buyers".
  - Cold Meta/TikTok broad prospecting → advertorial. Retargeting audiences that have seen the brand 3–4 times → listicle.
  - Problem-led listicles can reach colder audiences than product-led ones.
  - The article cites Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966) and NN/g scanning research as its basis. — [Landra, 2026](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/advertorial-vs-listicle-which-converts-better)
- Landra also says "compressed advertorials" are now winning "nearly as often as listicles even in categories once assumed to need the long story". These are lighter and shorter than the classic 2,000-word story. MARKETER CLAIM. — [Landra, 2026](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/advertorial-vs-listicle-which-converts-better)

**VSL vs text advertorial (nutra)**
- Daily Intel (May 2026) claims VSLs convert cold nutra traffic at "roughly 2.5x the rate of a comparable text advertorial". The figure comes from their internal tracking and is "a range, not a guarantee".
- Daily Intel says text advertorials win on native placements, with older demographics and on slow connections. They also survive Meta ad review better than a VSL sent straight to a sales page.
- An advertorial takes hours to a day to build. A VSL takes 3 days to 2+ weeks.
- Offers that are scaling often run advertorial → VSL. Nutra VSLs run 8–24 minutes (internal study of 306 VSLs). MARKETER CLAIM, unverified. — [Daily Intel Service, 2026](https://dailyintelservice.com/learn/vsl-vs-advertorial-which-converts-better-for-nutra)

**COD nutra in Eastern Europe and other Tier-2 markets**
- ClickDealer (Mar 2026) describes the standard nutra funnel as Ad → Prelander → Offer page → Checkout/lead form. Its notes on Tier-2 geos (Eastern Europe, LATAM):
  - moderate traffic costs and strong COD performance
  - users respond to emotional angles
  - localization makes a noticeable difference
  - "direct, benefit-based funnels with credibility indicators perform better than overly polished designs" — [ClickDealer, 2026](https://blog.clickdealer.com/a-z-affiliate-guide-to-promoting-nutra-offers/)
- A COD weight-loss prelander for Turkey (a comparable emerging COD market) used:
  - a doctor Q&A/interview format
  - before/after customer testimonials
  - limited-stock urgency
  - FAQ-style objection handling
  - The reviewer criticised it for having no product data or study, poor flow from interview to reviews, inconsistent photo styling, and testimonial faces that didn't look local. Published Apr 2022. — [CPA.RIP, 2022](https://cpa.rip/en/blogs/nutra-pre-landing-analysis/)

### Inferences
- For FitPatches' cold Meta traffic, the default is a story or "new mechanism" advertorial: either first-person testimonial or "expert explains". A shorter "N reasons why" listicle is the challenger for warm and retargeting audiences. Neither should be assumed to win; both are test candidates.
- The "doctor/expert discovers" format is common in Eastern-European COD nutra (see the Turkey example). On Meta it carries the highest policy risk if the expert is fictional or implies medical endorsement (see Section 4).
- Quiz funnels and VSLs are less likely to fit a low-ticket COD patch offer. LanderLab ties VSLs to offers over $500 and older audiences, and quizzes to lead-gen. Treat both as later tests.

### Gaps
- No public controlled study compares story advertorial vs listicle vs PDP on matched Meta traffic for health or weight loss. All "which format wins" statements are practitioner consensus.
- Not researched in this pass: format guidance from Atria, Foreplay, Swipe, Konrad Sanders, Shane Kilgore, Marketer's Ink, Ry Schwartz and Andre Chaperon. Chaperon is email-focused and probably not relevant.
- Founder-letter and "review/comparison" formats for weight-loss patches: no format-specific data found.

## 2. Section-by-section anatomy of a long-form story advertorial (word counts, CTA count)

### Takeaway
Sources agree on the order of sections:
1. A news-style headline that matches the ad
2. A story or education open with no product for the first few hundred words
3. A problem diagnosis or "new mechanism" (why past solutions failed / it's not your fault)
4. The criteria for a real solution
5. The product as the logical result
6. A proof stack
7. Objection handling or FAQ
8. The offer, guarantee and urgency, placed last
9. A clear CTA

Recommended lengths vary widely: about 800 words (consumer, Apexure), 600–1,200 (nutra, unverified), 2,000+ (GemPages). There is no sourced rule on CTA count. Common practice is several in-line CTAs plus a sticky CTA.

### Cited Findings
- AdLibrary's 6-step anatomy:
  1. Story or education open, with no product mention for "the first several hundred words"
  2. Mechanism explanation: why standard approaches fail
  3. Solution criteria, stated before the product reveal
  4. The product entering as the "inevitable answer"
  5. Proof stack: specific test results, before/after, named claims
  6. Offer (price, urgency, guarantee) placed last — [AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/advertorial-landing-pages)
- Landra says an advertorial "spends its first 200 to 300 words building that diagnosis before it ever names a product category". The reason: cold clicks haven't diagnosed their problem yet. — [Landra, 2026](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/advertorial-vs-listicle-which-converts-better)
- ClickDealer's prelander rules for nutra:
  - open with a hook about a specific problem or contradiction, not the product
  - make the first screen feel like content, not an ad
  - focus on ONE mechanism
  - put testimonials and credentials early to reduce skepticism
  - give simple instructions for getting the product — [ClickDealer, 2026](https://blog.clickdealer.com/a-z-affiliate-guide-to-promoting-nutra-offers/)
- Flexxable's 4-part frame:
  - **Headline**: attention-grabbing and "new-sy"
  - **Opening**: myth exposed, story, emotional hot points
  - **Transition**: introduce the expert or solution, plus proof
  - **Close**: an obvious CTA
  - Formatting: 1–2 sentence paragraphs, images with captions that "act as subheadings", CTA above the fold. MARKETER CLAIM. — [Flexxable](https://flexxable.com/comprehensive-guide-on-how-to-write-high-converting-advertorials/) (snippet)
- Apexure on length: 800–2,000 words is the standard range, about 800 for consumer products, 1,500–2,000 for B2B. — [Apexure, 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/advertorials-a-detailed-guide-to-design-and-best-practices)
- GemPages recommends 2,000+ words, an image roughly every 150 words, bold key phrases, at least 16pt body text, multiple CTAs ("Check Availability"), and a sticky CTA column for mobile. — [GemPages, Sep 2025](https://gempages.net/blogs/shopify/how-to-write-create-high-converting-advertorial-landing-page)
- CTA placement in Apexure's examples:
  - Reggie: CTAs inside each benefit section plus a sticky side banner
  - Jones Road: two CTAs, one of them sticky
  - DOOR3: a single click-to-call button
  - Earthling Co.: a final CTA banner with a free gift and 20% off — [Apexure, 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/advertorials-a-detailed-guide-to-design-and-best-practices)
- Stefan Georgi's RMBC method (Research → Mechanism → Brief → Copy) frames the mechanism in two parts. The "Unique Mechanism of the Problem" (UMP) explains why the prospect has the problem and why other things failed. The "Unique Mechanism of the Solution" (UMS) explains why this product works. — [Stefan Georgi, RMBC](https://www.stefanpaulgeorgi.com/the-rmbc-method-for-better-copy/) (snippet); [GitHub community RMBC skills repo (not official)](https://github.com/coleschaffer/copywritingskills-rmbc)
- Practitioner critique of a full advertorial funnel (HISTORICAL, 2019): Justin Goff called the advertorial copy "pretty good. You could tweak it but it won't bring in a massive win." He and Georgi saw a possible 2–5x profit increase in the upsell and order pages instead. The point is that the advertorial is one link in a chain. — [Bejakovic/Desert Kite, 2019](https://bejakovic.com/justin-goff-and-stefan-georgi-critique-my-advertorial-copy/)

### Inferences
Synthesised anatomy for a FitPatches long-form story advertorial. This is a working template built from the sources above, not a sourced standard.

1. **Publication framing and byline.** Article layout, author name and photo, date, "Advertisement/Реклама" label. About 0–30 words. Do not fake a real Bulgarian outlet (see Section 4).
2. **Headline and subhead.** Matches the ad hook word for word. News or curiosity style.
3. **Lead (≈150–300 words).** A story lead (a relatable Bulgarian woman aged 35–55) or a secret/discovery lead. No product.
4. **Problem agitation and failed solutions / "it's not your fault" (≈200–400 words).** Diets, gyms and pills failed because of the UMP. One example of a UMP angle: the blood-sugar/insulin swings mechanism. Be careful with Meta's personal-attribute rule.
5. **New mechanism reveal and discovery story (≈200–400 words).** Berberine and the AMPK/"metabolic switch" framing, then the delivery mechanism (the patch, no pills, no stomach upset). Here the UMS is "transdermal delivery".
6. **Product introduction (≈100–200 words).** Show the product being used.
7. **Proof (≈200–400 words).** Studies on berberine (be honest that most evidence is for oral berberine), testimonials, UGC. No unrepresentative results.
8. **Objection handling / FAQ (≈150–300 words).** Does it work? Is it safe? How do I use it? COD and "pay on delivery" reassurance.
9. **Offer, guarantee and real scarcity (≈100–200 words).** Bundle pricing, guarantee, COD and delivery time via Econt/Speedy.
10. **CTA.** About 3–5 in-line CTAs after the mechanism section, plus a mobile sticky bar.
11. **Optional real comments/reviews block and PS.**

Total: about 1,200–2,200 words for the long version, or about 600–1,000 for a "compressed" variant.

- For COD in Bulgaria, the offer block should spell out the pay-on-delivery process and delivery time. In COD the costly failures happen after the click: unconfirmed orders and parcels that are never picked up (see Section 6). Copy that sets honest expectations can raise the approval rate.

### Gaps
- No primary source from Georgi, Goff, Albuquerque or Schwalm specifying advertorial word counts or exact CTA numbers was found. The numbers above come from agencies and page builders.
- Evidence on comments sections and PS was not found in a sourced form.
- One unverified search snippet claimed "a typical nutra advertorial prelander runs 600 to 1200 words, opens with a customer story, includes two or three product images, cites a fake or real study, and ends with a single button to the offer". I could not trace it to a specific page, so it is not cited.

## 3. Headline formulas, lead types and copy frameworks (Schwartz, Georgi, Albuquerque, Goff, Brown, Masterson/Forde)

### Takeaway
The working frameworks are:
- **Masterson/Forde's six lead types** (HISTORICAL, 2011–12): Offer, Promise, Problem-Solution, Big Secret, Proclamation, Story. They run from direct to indirect, following Schwartz's awareness levels. Cold weight-loss traffic calls for indirect leads (Story, Secret, Proclamation).
- **Unique mechanism (UMP/UMS)** from Georgi and Todd Brown.
- **Evaldo Albuquerque's "One Belief"**: one new opportunity, reachable only through one new mechanism.

Justin Goff's lead rules: no throat-clearing, tease the mechanism rather than explaining it in the lead, and avoid opening with questions.

### Cited Findings
- *Great Leads* (Michael Masterson and John Forde, AWAI, released Jan 2012):
  - Six lead types: Offer, Promise, Problem-Solution, Big Secret, Proclamation, Story.
  - They are arranged along an awareness continuum. Direct leads (Offer, Promise) suit informed, eager prospects. Indirect leads (Secret, Proclamation, Story) suit cold, skeptical ones.
  - The book repeats the claim that "80% of an ad's success comes from the headline and lead". MARKETER CLAIM, unsourced. — [AWAI, 2012](https://www.awai.com/2012/01/just-released-great-leads/); [Samuel J. Woods summary](https://samueljwoods.com/great-leads-the-six-easiest-ways-to-start-any-sales-message-by-michael-masterson-john-forde/) (snippet)
- Eugene Schwartz's five awareness stages (*Breakthrough Advertising*, 1966; HISTORICAL classic) are the basis DTC practitioners still use to choose between advertorial, listicle and PDP. — [Landra, 2026](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/advertorial-vs-listicle-which-converts-better)
- Evaldo Albuquerque, *The 16-Word Sales Letter*:
  - Copy starts by defining "One Belief", formula: "This new opportunity is the key to their desire and it's attainable only through my new mechanism."
  - The copy then answers 10 questions.
  - He says it applies to sales letters, emails, advertorials and FB/YT ads. — [Justin Charnell review](https://justincharnell.com/review/16-word-sales-letter-evaldo-albuquerque/); [Mack's Book Notes](https://macksbooknotes.com/the-16-word-sales-letter-evaldo-albuquerque/) (snippet)
- Stefan Georgi:
  - RMBC = Research, Mechanism, Brief, Copy.
  - The unique mechanism is "always a two-part logical connection": the mechanism behind the problem and the mechanism behind the solution.
  - He claims $700M+ in attributed client results. MARKETER CLAIM. — [GitHub RMBC repo (community, not official)](https://github.com/coleschaffer/copywritingskills-rmbc); [Stefan Georgi site](https://www.stefanpaulgeorgi.com/the-rmbc-method-for-better-copy/) (snippet)
- Todd Brown (E5 Method, Copy Chief Ep. 120; HISTORICAL, about 2017–2019):
  - The unique mechanism is "what is different about the way your product… is able to solve that problem".
  - The E5 campaign introduces a new idea, presents it as a new way to get results, explains how and why the mechanism works, then offers the mechanism through the product. — [Copy Chief Ep 120](https://copychief.com/ep-120-todd-brown/); [TAM podcast Ep 120](https://tam.libsyn.com/ep-120-todd-brown-the-e5-method-for-marketing-the-big-idea) (snippet)
- Justin Goff on leads:
  - Catch attention with curiosity, call out the pain point, and promise a unique, credible solution.
  - Don't "bury the lead" or "throat-clear".
  - Don't explain how the solution works in the lead ("over-explainer mode"). Give a "big sexy tease" and move on.
  - Avoid opening with questions, because they "signal selling" and sound like a radio ad. — [Bejakovic / Justin Goff archive](https://bejakovic.com/tag/justin-goff/) (snippet)
- Native-network headline data (HISTORICAL, older Outbrain research): headlines with negative superlatives had a 63% higher average CTR than positive ones. — [Brax, Outbrain best practices](https://www.brax.io/blog/outbrain-best-practices) (snippet)
- Native-network headline data: dynamic city/country placeholders in Taboola/Outbrain titles lift CTR 20–25%. This applies to native widgets, not Meta. MARKETER CLAIM. — [native-advertising.net](https://native-advertising.net/blog/dynamic-titles-taboola-outbrain-ctr) (snippet)

### Inferences
- For FitPatches, a One-Belief statement might read: "Steadying blood-sugar swings is the key to finally losing stubborn weight after 35, and it's now possible without pills through a berberine patch that releases slowly all day." It must be phrased within Meta's and EU health-claim rules.
- Headline and lead types worth testing for cold Meta traffic:
  - Story lead: "How a 47-year-old teacher from Plovdiv…"
  - Secret/discovery lead: "The 'metabolic switch' plant compound doctors call 'nature's…'". Careful: unverifiable comparisons to drugs are high-risk.
  - Proclamation/news lead: "New patch format makes berberine…"
- Negative framing ("the #1 mistake women over 40 make…") may lift CTR, as the native data suggests. But Meta's personal-attributes rule restricts second-person negative body framing in the ad itself. Keep negative superlatives off the person ("the mistake most diets make") rather than "your belly fat".
- Georgi's UMP/UMS maps onto berberine like this. The UMP is blood-sugar/insulin-driven fat storage, plus pills that cause stomach issues or are forgotten. The UMS is steady transdermal delivery. The UMS claim needs honest evidence; see Section 4 on claim risk.

### Gaps
- Not researched with primary sources in this pass: Gary Halbert headline examples, Kim Krause Schwalm's health-copy guidance, and Copyhackers/Marketing Examples headline data. No 2022–2026 quantitative study on which headline types win in health advertorials was found.

## 4. Design/UX elements that lift conversion, and compliance risk (comments, timers, stock counters, "news" framing)

### Takeaway
The only hard data found concerns attention and speed:
- **NN/g (2018):** 57% of viewing time is above the fold and 74% within the first two screens. So the headline, hero image and lead carry most of the load, and CTAs repeated lower down catch the minority who read on.
- **Google/55/Deloitte (2020):** a 0.1-second mobile speed gain raised retail conversions 8.4%.

Everything else (an image every ~150 words, captions, bold text, sticky CTAs) is practitioner best practice.

The compliance risk sits in the "fake" elements: invented news outlets or doctors, fake comment sections, false scarcity and countdown timers. Under the EU Omnibus Directive and Meta's misleading-claims policy these are real risks for a Bulgarian brand.

### Cited Findings
**Attention and layout (HARD DATA)**
- NN/g eyetracking (Apr 2018):
  - 57% of page-viewing time above the fold, down from 80% in 2010
  - 74% within the first two screenfuls
  - more than 42% within the top 20% of the page and more than 65% within the top 40%
  - "a sharp decrease in attention following the fold"
  - Guidance: put priority content and CTAs high, use headers and bold to guide attention, avoid "false floors". HISTORICAL-ish (2018). — [NN/g, 2018](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- Mobile speed: the "Milliseconds Make Millions" study (Google, 55 and Deloitte, 2020) covered 37 European and US brand sites and over 30M sessions. A 0.1s faster mobile site raised retail conversions 8.4% and retail AOV 9.2%. HARD DATA, but commissioned by Google. — [web.dev case study](https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions)

**Design best practices (MARKETER CLAIMS)**
- An image about every 150 words, at least 16pt body text, bold key phrases, headers, bullets, short paragraphs, and a sticky CTA for mobile. The hero image should show the product or a before/after. — [GemPages, 2025](https://gempages.net/blogs/shopify/how-to-write-create-high-converting-advertorial-landing-page)
- Bylines with author photos add credibility. Lifestyle and "in-use" shots beat isolated product shots (Javy Coffee example). Test sticky vs non-sticky CTAs. Track scroll depth, time on page and bounce alongside conversions. — [Apexure, 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/advertorials-a-detailed-guide-to-design-and-best-practices)
- Captions act as subheadings because skimmers read them, and paragraphs should be 1–2 sentences. — [Flexxable](https://flexxable.com/comprehensive-guide-on-how-to-write-high-converting-advertorials/) (snippet)
- Tier-2/Eastern-European COD audiences respond better to "direct, benefit-based funnels with credibility indicators" than to "overly polished designs". Localization matters. — [ClickDealer, 2026](https://blog.clickdealer.com/a-z-affiliate-guide-to-promoting-nutra-offers/)
- In a COD weight-loss prelander review, testimonial faces that didn't look local and inconsistent photo styles were flagged as conversion weaknesses. — [CPA.RIP, 2022](https://cpa.rip/en/blogs/nutra-pre-landing-analysis/)

**Meta policy (Meta reviews the landing page too)**
- Meta reviews the ad creative, all text fields AND the destination page. An ad can be rejected for a policy violation on the landing page alone. — [AuditSocials, 2026](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-ad-misleading-claims-personal-attributes-prohibited-content-policy-2026) (snippet); [Stackmatix, 2026](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/meta-ads-policy) (snippet)
- Meta's misleading-claims triggers, according to third-party guides (snippets):
  - creative that "fakes an editorial or news context"
  - before/after weight-loss claims implying typical results
  - unrepresentative testimonials
  - "false scarcity and countdown timers"
  - implied celebrity or brand endorsement, which is "high-severity" and can restrict the whole account
  - Sources: [AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-ad-misleading-claims-personal-attributes-prohibited-content-policy-2026); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/meta-ads-policy); [bir.ch](https://bir.ch/blog/what-can-you-advertise-on-meta)
- Weight-loss products must be targeted to people aged 18+. Second-person body framing such as "tired of your belly fat?" breaks the personal-attributes rule. Pinched-fat imagery and sensational health claims remain banned. — [AuditSocials weight-loss guide, 2026](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-health-wellness-restricted-ads-2026-supplements-body-image-medical-claim-rules) (snippet)
- Reported July 2026 policy change, UNVERIFIED against Meta's own pages: third parties report that on 22 July 2026 Meta rewrote its Health & Wellness standards from product-based to claims-based enforcement. Their account:
  - before/after imagery for weight loss is "no longer auto-rejected" and violates only when paired with prohibited claims
  - the 18+ requirement remains for products claiming weight loss
  - landing pages should drop "inferiority framing, guaranteed outcomes, and timeline promises"
  - Clikim does not link to Meta's official text. — [Clikim, 22 Jul 2026](https://clikim.com/meta-health-wellness-policy-update/); [AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-beauty-cosmetics-ads-2026-before-after-photos-appearance-claims-policy) (snippet)
- My attempts to read Meta's official Health & Wellness policy pages failed: the Transparency Center URL returned 404, and the Business Help page returned only its title. — [Meta Business Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/2489235377779939)

**EU law (applies to Bulgaria)**
- The EU Omnibus Directive (Directive (EU) 2019/2161), applicable since 28 May 2022:
  - bans submitting, or commissioning others to submit, fake consumer reviews or endorsements, including social-media "likes"
  - bans misrepresenting reviews (Annex I, item 23c)
  - bans claiming reviews come from real users or buyers without "reasonable and proportionate steps" to verify this (item 23b)
  - applies to any trader targeting EU consumers — [Makeinfluence](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/fake-and-manipulated-reviews-what-the-eu-omnibus-directive-bans); [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/fake-reviews-crackdown-in-europe-and-beyond); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1189542/the-omnibus-directive-consumer-reviews) (snippets)
- Apexure says reputable platforms require advertorials to carry labels such as "Sponsored", "Paid Content" or "Advertisement" (an FTC-style norm; Apexure gives no legal citation). — [Apexure, 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/advertorials-a-detailed-guide-to-design-and-best-practices)

### Inferences
- **Above the fold on mobile**: headline matching the ad, a subhead with the promise, a byline with date, a hero image (person or product in use), and the first 2–3 lines of the lead. Given NN/g's 57%/74% split, test these first.
- **Elements to keep**: an image every ~100–150 words with captions written as mini-headlines; bold for the 1–2 key sentences per screen; a mobile sticky CTA bar that appears after the mechanism section; repeated in-line CTAs; local Bulgarian faces, names and towns in testimonials, used only with permission and from real customers.
- **Elements that are high-risk for FitPatches** (Meta account plus Bulgarian/EU consumer law):
  - a fake Facebook-style comment section with invented users
  - countdown timers that reset
  - fake stock counters ("only 7 left")
  - a masthead imitating a real news site (e.g., copying the look of a real Bulgarian outlet)
  - a fictional "Dr. ___"
  - "as seen on" claims that aren't true
  - Real comments, real reviews, honest scarcity (e.g., a real launch discount end date) and an "Реклама/Advertisement" label are the safe versions.
- **Speed**: Bulgarian mobile traffic arrives from inside the Facebook/Instagram in-app browser. Lightweight pages (compressed images, minimal scripts) are directly supported by the 0.1s → +8.4% finding. That study was on retail sites, not advertorials, so the exact effect is not established.
- **Copy style**: given ClickDealer's Tier-2 note, a plain, credible "real article" look may beat a heavily designed brand page for this market. Test it rather than assume.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on: the lift from exit pops, progress bars, timers or stock counters on advertorials; target readability grade (e.g., grade 5–7); NN/g mobile-specific scroll data for long articles; Baymard/CXL advertorial studies.
- Meta's July 2026 policy change needs checking against Meta's own Transparency Center text before the report relies on it.
- The UCPD (Directive 2005/29/EC) Annex I ban on paid editorial content that isn't disclosed as advertising, and how Bulgaria's Consumer Protection Act and its regulator (the Consumer Protection Commission, КЗП) enforce it, were not researched here. They are likely relevant to "news-style" advertorials and should be checked.
- EU/Bulgarian rules on health claims for berberine (Regulation 1924/2006; berberine has no authorised EU health claim, as far as I know) were not researched here and need separate verification.

## 5. Ad-to-advertorial "scent" (message match) and the role of the advertorial in the funnel

### Takeaway
The advertorial's job is to carry the ad's argument forward. Same hook, same mechanism, same visual. It builds the one belief the reader needs, handles objections, and pre-frames the offer before the reader sees the price. Message match is the most-repeated rule. A presell page that gets clicks but no purchases on the offer page is failing, so the advertorial is judged by downstream CPA, not by click-out.

### Cited Findings
- AdLibrary's message-match questions before launch:
  - Is the ad's angle recognisable on the page?
  - Does the same mechanism continue?
  - Is the product visually and verbally consistent?
  - Is the promised proof actually there?
  - Is the next step obvious?
- AdLibrary frames the decision as "whether the destination keeps the ad's argument alive". It also suggests pulling competitors' ads to see which destination types they validate at scale. — [AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/advertorial-landing-pages)
- LanderLab: the advertorial headline should match the ad. "A presell page that generates clicks but no conversions on the offer is failing even if the CTR looks good… the metric that matters is cost per acquisition." — [LanderLab, 2026](https://landerlab.io/blog/presell-page-examples)
- The prelander gives room to present angles and USPs before asking for the purchase. Its first screen should feel like content, not an ad. — [ClickDealer, 2026](https://blog.clickdealer.com/a-z-affiliate-guide-to-promoting-nutra-offers/)
- Daily Intel says advertorials reduce ad-review exposure. A news-styled text page "can carry disclaimers, softer claims, and editorial framing", which is why advertorials survive Meta review better than a VSL linked directly. MARKETER CLAIM. — [Daily Intel, 2026](https://dailyintelservice.com/learn/vsl-vs-advertorial-which-converts-better-for-nutra)
- The advertorial is one link in the chain. In the 2019 Goff/Georgi critique, the biggest potential gain (2–5x profit) was in the upsell and order pages, not the advertorial. HISTORICAL. — [Bejakovic, 2019](https://bejakovic.com/justin-goff-and-stefan-georgi-critique-my-advertorial-copy/)
- One vendor claims that pointing the same ads at an advertorial instead of the PDP "cut customer acquisition cost by nearly half" (unnamed brand). VENDOR CLAIM. — [Landra, 2026](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/advertorial-vs-listicle-which-converts-better)

### Inferences
- For FitPatches, build one advertorial per main ad angle (blood sugar/cravings, "no pills", menopause belly, a testimonial story). Keep a 1:1 match between the ad hook and the advertorial headline and hero, rather than one generic advertorial for every ad.
- Price pre-framing for COD: make the cost of failed alternatives concrete before the price appears (gym membership, pills, wasted months), and spell out the low risk of paying on delivery. Because the order is only paid on delivery, the advertorial should set realistic expectations to protect parcel pickup rates.
- Track a message-match KPI: compare advertorial bounce rate and scroll depth per ad. If one ad's visitors bounce much more, the "scent" is broken.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of the conversion effect of message match on advertorials was found. The rule is practitioner consensus.

## 6. Benchmarks: advertorial CTR to offer, scroll depth, time on page, CVR, and advertorial vs direct-to-PDP

### Takeaway
There is no reliable, method-backed public benchmark for "advertorial → PDP click-through rate". The numbers found range from 3–8% to 20–40%, depend on the traffic source and the definition used, and are almost all marketer claims. The same is true of "2–3x conversion vs PDP", "+77% ROAS" and "CAC cut in half". The best course is to treat external numbers as rough orientation and set FitPatches' own baselines. Judge advertorials on cost per approved (and delivered) COD order, and on revenue or profit per visitor, not on click-out.

### Cited Findings
**Advertorial → offer CTR ("CTR out")**
| Claim | Context | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| CTA click rates of 3–8% on "good advertorials"; average engagement time 2–4 minutes | general advertorials | MARKETER CLAIM, no method | [Apexure, 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/advertorials-a-detailed-guide-to-design-and-best-practices) |
| Advertorial/prelander CTR improved "from 20% to 40%", landing-page CVR from 3% to 12% | DTC optimisation (agency page title) | MARKETER CLAIM | [Stealthlabz](https://stealthlabz.com/topics/dtc-operations/dtc-conversion-optimization) (snippet) |
| 10–20% CTR is "very good" for pre-landers | mobile sweepstakes, affiliate | MARKETER CLAIM; different vertical | [Mobidea](https://www.mobidea.com/academy/purelander-review/) (snippet) |
| Placements bringing 80% of leads had LP CTR ≥8%; rule: cut placements that spent >$2 with LP CTR <7% | Zeropark push/pop, sweepstakes | affiliate case study; different vertical | [iAmAttila](https://iamattila.com/super-affiliate-tips/how-to-guides/sweeps-camps-zeropark-push) (snippet) |
| Breakeven on a $47 CPA offer needed 4.1% pre-lander CTR on Taboola and 3.6% on Outbrain | native traffic | MARKETER CLAIM, single example | [Affiliate Times, 2026](https://affiliate-times.com/taboola-vs-outbrain-in-2026-which-native-ad-platform-wins-for-affiliates-5/) (snippet) |

- These figures are not comparable. Some measure unique clicks / unique visitors, some clicks / pageviews, some exclude bounces. Traffic sources differ: push and pop vs Meta vs native.

**Advertorial vs direct-to-PDP**
- A dedicated pre-sell page "converts 2–3x higher than a product page". Paid traffic sent straight to a PDP converts at about 2% (1.8–2.5%). VENDOR CLAIMS, no method published. — [Landra, 2026](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/advertorial-vs-listicle-which-converts-better); [Convertibles](https://convertibles.dev/blogs/optimization/examples-of-advertorials) (snippets)
- A presell page "can double or triple conversion rates" vs sending traffic straight to the sales page. MARKETER CLAIM. — [LanderLab, 2026](https://landerlab.io/blog/presell-page-examples)
- Tier 11 (agency) reports presell pages increased ROAS by 77%. Agency case study, details not public. — [LanderLab citing Tier 11](https://landerlab.io/blog/presell-page-examples)
- Same ads pointed at an advertorial instead of the PDP "cut CAC by nearly half". Vendor, unnamed brand. — [Landra, 2026](https://www.getlandra.com/blog/advertorial-vs-listicle-which-converts-better)
- VSLs convert cold nutra traffic at about 2.5x a comparable text advertorial (internal tracking). — [Daily Intel, 2026](https://dailyintelservice.com/learn/vsl-vs-advertorial-which-converts-better-for-nutra)
- Basic maths: a 15% conversion-rate gain gives 15% more purchases and about 13% lower CAC (1/1.15). — [Trent Turner, 2024](https://trentturner.substack.com/p/5-landing-pages-that-are-worth-testing)

**Attention and speed (HARD DATA, not advertorial-specific)**
- 57% of viewing time above the fold and 74% in the first two screens (NN/g, 2018). — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)
- 0.1s faster mobile load → +8.4% retail conversions (2020). — [web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/milliseconds-make-millions)

**COD-specific metrics**
- With COD, the order counts only after call-centre confirmation, which creates an "approval rate bottleneck". Track approval rate and cost per approved order, because "front-end performance isn't the full picture". — [ClickDealer, 2026](https://blog.clickdealer.com/a-z-affiliate-guide-to-promoting-nutra-offers/)
- Context for ad-level CTR: LocalIQ/WordStream report an average Facebook traffic-campaign CTR of 1.93% across industries. This is ad CTR, not advertorial CTR. — [LocalIQ, 2026](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/) (snippet)

### Inferences
Working ranges for FitPatches, to be replaced by its own data. These come from my reading of the sources, not from a sourced benchmark.

**Metric definitions and dashboard**
- **Advertorial CTR out** = unique clicks to offer / unique advertorial sessions. Watch it per ad and per variant. Single-digit CTR out on cold Meta traffic is a warning sign. Low double digits is workable. The 20–40% figures claimed by agencies are reported as "optimised" results, not typical ones.
- **Offer-page CVR** (order form submits / offer-page sessions) and **end-to-end CVR** (orders / advertorial sessions).
- **COD funnel**: orders → confirmed (approval rate) → shipped → delivered and collected (buyout rate). The decision metric is cost per *delivered* order, or profit per advertorial visitor.
- **Engagement diagnostics**: scroll depth to the first CTA and to the offer block, median engaged time. Apexure's "2–4 min" is an unsourced reference point.

**Making the advertorial vs PDP decision**
- Run a split at ad-set or URL level: the same creative sent to the advertorial and to the PDP.
- Compare CPA per delivered order and revenue per click. Don't compare raw CVR, because the advertorial adds a step.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, method-backed dataset of advertorial CTR-out, scroll depth or time on page for health or weight-loss offers on Meta.
- No public data on Bulgarian or Eastern-European COD approval or buyout rates for advertorial vs direct funnels. This may be covered by another research stream.
- Mobidea, iAmAttila, Affiliate Times and Stealthlabz figures were seen only in search snippets. Their exact context and definitions were not verified.
- Unverified snippet: "Advertorials typically show an 80 to 150% conversion rate uplift versus direct offers". I could not attribute it to a specific page, so it is not cited.

## 7. How top operators A/B test advertorials (what to test first, sample sizes, metrics, tools, cadence)

### Takeaway
Test big things first: angle/mechanism and format, then headline, hero image and lead (the parts above the fold that most visitors see), then offer and CTA. Test *materially different* versions, not cosmetic tweaks. Judge on downstream metrics (CPA, revenue per visitor, and for COD the cost per delivered order), not on CTR out alone. Specific guidance on sample size and testing cadence from named operators was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Run at least two variants to statistical significance. Track downstream conversions on the offer page, not just CTR. Segment by presell variant to get CPA per variant. Match format to traffic source. — [LanderLab, 2026](https://landerlab.io/blog/presell-page-examples)
- Test story-driven vs direct pitch, use curiosity gaps, A/B test sticky vs non-sticky CTAs, vary listicle vs article by industry. Track scroll depth, time on page and bounce rate alongside conversions. — [Apexure, 2026](https://www.apexure.com/blog/advertorials-a-detailed-guide-to-design-and-best-practices)
- Revenue per visitor is a better primary test metric than conversion rate because it includes AOV. That matters when bundles (1/2/3 packs) are on offer. — [Omniconvert](https://www.omniconvert.com/what-is/revenue-per-visitor-rpv/) (snippet)
- GemPages says successful advertorial operators test "40+ monthly variations", and that a "6 reasons why" format did exceptionally well. MARKETER CLAIM, context unclear. — [GemPages, 2025](https://gempages.net/blogs/shopify/how-to-write-create-high-converting-advertorial-landing-page)
- Before launching, check message match and pull competitors' ads to see which destination types they validate at scale. — [AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/posts/advertorial-landing-pages)
- Native networks: test many headlines and images to keep CTR up, and test unfamiliar landing-page types. Reading results: high CTR with low CPA means scale; high CTR with high CPA means the landing page or offer is broken. — [Brax/Outbrain best practices](https://www.brax.io/blog/outbrain-best-practices) (snippet)
- Improve the whole funnel. Goff and Georgi expected more upside from the order and upsell pages than from advertorial tweaks. HISTORICAL. — [Bejakovic, 2019](https://bejakovic.com/justin-goff-and-stefan-georgi-critique-my-advertorial-copy/)
- The attention data supports prioritising above-the-fold tests: 57% of viewing time above the fold. — [NN/g, 2018](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)

### Inferences
Suggested test order for FitPatches. This is a synthesis, not a sourced protocol.
1. **Destination format**: advertorial vs listicle vs PDP, using the same ad.
2. **Angle/mechanism**: blood sugar vs cravings vs "no pills" patch vs menopause.
3. **Headline plus hero image.**
4. **Lead type**: story vs secret/discovery.
5. **Offer framing on the offer page**: bundles, guarantee, COD reassurance.
6. **Proof blocks and CTA mechanics**: sticky bar, CTA copy.

**Statistics**
- Bulgarian traffic volumes are small. Use big-swing tests and judge by CPA per delivered order with enough conversions per arm.
- A common heuristic among practitioners is at least 50–100 conversions per variant for purchase-level decisions. I did not find a source for this in this pass, so treat it as unsourced.
- With low volumes, CTR out and scroll depth can act as early leading indicators to kill clear losers.

**Tools**
- Microsoft Clarity (free heatmaps and session recordings) for scroll and drop-off points.
- Server-side split by URL in Meta (separate ads or ad sets) or a page-builder split test.
- UTM/ad-ID tracking carried through to the COD order sheet, so that approval and buyout rates can be attributed to each advertorial variant.

### Gaps
- No sourced guidance from named operators (Atria, Foreplay, Thesis/Swipe, CTC, Pilothouse, Foxwell, Hustler Marketing) on sample sizes, test cadence or heatmap workflows was found in this pass.
- Microsoft Clarity and Hotjar usage for advertorials is not documented in the sources fetched. The tool suggestions above are general CRO practice, not cited.
