## Q1. Which brands and pages advertise slimming/appetite patches or berberine most heavily in BG/RO/GR/DE/PL, which ads have run longest, and where do they link?

### Takeaway
In Bulgaria the most active direct competitor is **Smile Patches** (smilepatchesbg.com, a Bulgarian company, "Смайл Пачес ЕООД"). It has about 192 active ads, a listicle advertorial, creator (branded-content) ads and a 2+1 / 3+2 offer that undercuts FitPatches. Behind it come VitaPatch.bg (a multi-formula patch brand from Pleven), Alpine Patches (a pan-EU "Swiss" funnel) and a long-running Italian-style nutra COD lander (Herbolario Raíz Verde, live since 8 July 2026).

In DE/RO/PL/GR the space is dominated by two groups:
- **Advertorial funnels**: FreshHaut / VitaHaut Klinik with "Slimora", and WeightWorld as the "legit retailer" benchmark.
- **A cross-border network of fake "Dr." persona pages** paying in USD, which push nano-microneedle "Moringa + Berberin" patches. These ads churn within hours or days, and Meta has already disabled at least one of the pages.

### Inferences
- **Strongest benchmarks for FitPatches, in priority order:**
  1. **Smile Patches**: same product (berberine + nar + канела + B + L-глутамин + хром), same market, a heavier and more professional creative engine, and currently a cheaper bundle.
  2. **VitaPatch.bg**: a well-built local brand with clean legal and trust blocks, mix-and-match and a quiz.
  3. **Alpine Patches**: a polished multi-country funnel with COD.
  4. **Herbolario Raíz Verde**: an aggressive Italian-style COD lander that survives for months.
  5. **FreshHaut / Slimora**: the DE advertorial funnel template.
  6. **WeightWorld.de**: the "legit retailer" pattern.
  7. **forlife.bg**: the BG supplement DTC trust pattern.
- **Run time is a weak proxy for profit in this niche.** Most DE/RO/PL "Dr." persona ads run for hours, consistent with heavy creative churn or account bans. The only multi-month survivors seen were Herbolario's GLP-1 BG ad (since 8 Jul) and Smile Patches' listicle and creator ads (since 4 and 10 Sep).
- **Smile Patches' near-daily batches of 20+ ads with identical link titles** suggest broad dynamic-creative testing on Advantage+ with a large budget.
- **The persona-page tactic is already being punished by Meta.** The Dobrescu page was disabled. FitPatches' own use of a persona page ("Елена Василева") carries similar risk.

### Gaps
- The API returns no spend, impressions (except "<100" flags on some ad pages), or end dates. The longest-running ranking is therefore based on the start dates of active ads seen in sampled ad pages, not a full sort.
- Logged-out Ad Library keyword search could not be scraped (it redirects to login), so ranking by impressions was impossible.
- Several Ad Library pages loaded empty because of Meta throttling: Smile Patches' oldest active ads (e.g. 1133178395802386), Dr. Evelina Martin's ad and SenseFit's ad. Their landing pages are unknown.
- FreshHaut's own website domain was not found. The ads go to hildas-waren.de (a store name that differs from the page name).

## Q2. What offer patterns are used in the region (bundles, free units, COD vs card, free-shipping thresholds, guarantees, per-pack prices, upsells, phone confirmation, one-step COD forms vs Shopify checkout)?

### Takeaway
The regional standard for patches is **1 / 2+1 / 3+2** (pay 2 get 3, pay 3 get 5), with the per-day price shown (€0.28–0.31/day at the top tier). Other common elements:
- free shipping tied to the middle or top bundle
- COD shown first
- a 30–60-day (Bulgaria) or 60–180-day (persona networks) "money-back" promise
- a **name + phone "quick order / we will call you" form** next to the cart

FitPatches' tiers are below the market on value: 2 packs for €27.99 versus Smile Patches' 3 packs for €26.99 and VitaPatch's 3 packs for €27.80.

## Q4. What do Bulgarian and CEE buyers expect for trust and checkout, what are typical COD refusal rates, and how do sellers reduce them?

### Takeaway
COD is still the default in Bulgaria. It was **50.3% of online payments in 2024** and about 48% in 2025 per the Bulgarian E-commerce Association (BEA), and **92.9% of BG stores offer it**. Romania is similar or higher (66% of orders per EuroCommerce, as reported by Shopilo).

Self-reported fulfilment data puts **successful-delivery rates for supplements at 92–97% in Bulgaria and 90–95% in Romania**. Refusal-reduction levers are:
- pre-dispatch confirmation calls (+4–7 pp)
- SMS sequences
- office/locker delivery
- realistic delivery dates
- "преглед преди плащане" / "Отвори и тествай"
- blacklists or partial prepayment for high-risk orders

Trust signals that BG competitors put above the fold:
- COD wording ("Плащане при доставка / наложен платеж")
- Econt/Speedy and 1–2-day delivery
- review count
- money-back guarantee (30–60 days)
- a BG phone number and quick-order call-back
- company data (ЕИК, БАБХ number for supplements)

### Inferences
- **For a €15–40 supplement-type parcel in Bulgaria, a well-run COD operation should expect roughly 3–8% refusals** (92–97% delivered, per the BigArena self-reported range). Rates in the low teens would indicate weak lead quality: impulse clicks from aggressive ads, persona or doctor pages, or unconfirmed orders. Confirmation calls before dispatch are the single most-cited lever.
- **Bulgarian buyers expect to see near the CTA:**
  - "Плащане при доставка", and ideally "преглед преди плащане"
  - an Econt/Speedy logo or name and a concrete delivery date
  - a Bulgarian mobile number with a "we'll call you" option
  - a money-back promise
  - the company name with ЕИК
  For supplements, a БАБХ registration number is also expected. Note: patches are often positioned as cosmetic ("козметичен продукт за външна употреба", Slim-patches), and the correct registration route is not covered in these notes.
- **Euro display is now standard** among competitors (€ everywhere except Max Active's "89лв" threshold). Cleanly displayed euro prices, possibly with лв for reassurance during the transition, are a minor trust point.
- **Fake media logos (bTV/NOVA) and "−10 кг" claims appear only on foreign nutra landers.** The leading local brands (Smile Patches, VitaPatch, forlife) rely instead on review volume, company data, BG courier cues and EU-authorised claims. This suggests the Bulgarian market rewards "local and legitimate" over "miracle".

### Gaps
- No official Econt or Speedy statistics on COD refusal rates were found. Every refusal figure here comes from vendor or fulfilment-company blogs without disclosed methodology (xlog.bg, BigArena, bezraboten.net).
- BEA's own report PDF was not accessed. The figures come from secondary coverage (BIA, Ecommerce Europe, Enterprise.bg). The COD share conflicts with DIGIPAY / Balkan Summit (48–50% vs 32%).
- No source quantified refusal rates specifically for patches or weight-loss products in CEE.
- Whether berberine patches sold in Bulgaria need БАБХ notification (as food supplements) or are treated as cosmetics was not researched here.
