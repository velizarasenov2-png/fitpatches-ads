# CRO Evidence and EU/Bulgarian Compliance Limits for a DTC Wellness Store (FitPatches, berberine patches, BG, Meta traffic, mostly COD)

Research date: 2 October 2026. Source-quality tags used throughout:

## A1. Quantity-break bundles / tiered offers (AOV, CR, number of tiers, anchoring, "most popular", per-day framing, free gift vs discount)

### Takeaway
Independent academic evidence supports the *mechanisms* behind a 3-tier bundle (middle-option/compromise preference, "free units" beating equivalent % discounts, per-day reframing of small totals), but the widely quoted AOV uplift numbers (+20–40%, +30% RPV) are vendor/blog claims with no audited methodology. A "3+1 gift" framing is better supported by research than a "-66%" framing, and it is also legally safer than a %-off-a-fake-reference-price.

### Cited Findings
- **Bonus pack ("X% more free") vs equivalent price discount:** consumers neglect the base value of percentages and therefore prefer bonus packs (e.g., "50% more free") over economically equivalent price discounts (e.g., ~33–35% off) — Chen, Marmorstein, Tsiros & Rao, *Journal of Marketing* 2012, "When More Is Less: The Impact of Base Value Neglect on Consumer Preferences for Bonus Packs over Price Discounts" **[ACADEMIC]** — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2763213); [summary](https://kenthendricks.com/discounts-vs-bonuses/)
- **Per-day ("pennies-a-day") framing:** reframing an aggregate price as a small per-day amount makes people compare it to small everyday expenses and raises acceptance; e.g., a donation framed as "85 cents per day" was accepted by 52% of those asked. The effect **reverses for large amounts** ("$1/day" beats "$365/year", but "$4,200/year" beats "$11.50/day") — Gourville, *Journal of Consumer Research* 1998 **[ACADEMIC]** — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:v:24:y:1998:i:4:p:395-408); [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-science-behind-behavior/201904/the-powerful-influence-pennies-day-price-offers)
- **Middle option preference:** items positioned in the centre of an array are chosen more often (centre-stage effect, Valenzuela & Raghubir); and options in the "middle" of an attribute range gain share (compromise effect, Simonson 1989) **[ACADEMIC]** — [ResearchGate: Position-based beliefs: the center-stage effect](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/247353185_Position-based_beliefs_The_center-stage_effect); [Kim 2022, Psychology & Marketing](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mar.21720)
- **"Recommended" label null result:** in a large experiment, labelling the option as the "recommended" choice did **not** significantly increase acceptance (context: upsell of a data-protection plan, not a supplement bundle) — Luguri & Strahilevitz, *Journal of Legal Analysis* 2021 **[ACADEMIC]** — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jla/article/13/1/43/6180579)
- **Price per unit is a known PDP gap:** 81% of benchmarked sites omit price-per-unit information for multi-quantity products; 67% lack a total-cost estimate near the buy section (Baymard 2026 Product Page benchmark) **[INDEPENDENT]** — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Bundles "can boost AOV by 20–40%"; bundle-purchase AOV "runs 40–60% higher than single-item purchases" **[BLOG, no primary data]** — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/ecommerce-growth/product-bundling)
- "A/B-tested bundle deals earned about 30% more revenue per visitor" than untested ones; recommends running two tier configurations for 2–3 weeks **[VENDOR – Shopify app]** — [Oxify](https://oxify.app/blog/quantity-break-strategies-aov)
- Bundle apps commonly ship "Most Popular" / "Best Value" labels as standard UI (descriptive, not evidence of effect) **[VENDOR]** — [Barn2](https://barn2.com/blog/shopify-quantity-discount-apps/)

### Inferences
- For FitPatches' 1 / 2 / 3+1 selector: keep 3 tiers, put the target tier (likely 2 or 3+1) in the middle/visually highlighted position, and express the top tier as "+1 box free" (bonus-pack framing) rather than "Спестяваш 66%". This also sidesteps the 30-day-prior-price rule if the gift is not presented as a % reduction on a prior price (see B1 caveat).
- Show price **per day / per patch** under each tier (patches are low-ticket, so per-day framing should help per Gourville; it would hurt only for large totals). Baymard's 81% gap suggests this is an easy differentiator.
- "Most popular" must be literally true (it is a factual claim under UCPD Art. 6). If it is not based on real order data, label the tier "Best value" (objectively verifiable from per-unit price) instead.
- A real test is needed: since evidence for the size of uplift is vendor-only, run an A/B (or sequential) test on tier order/default-selected tier.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) controlled study found quantifying AOV/CR lift from quantity-break bundles specifically in supplements, or the optimal number of tiers (3 vs 4).
- No independent data comparing "free gift" vs "free extra unit" vs "% discount" in DTC wellness specifically (Chen et al. was in grocery-type goods).

## A2. Subscriptions in supplements (adoption, LTV) and whether they work with cash on delivery

### Takeaway
Subscription benchmarks for supplements come almost entirely from agency/vendor blogs (monthly churn ~4–12%, heavy month-1 churn). No source was found on running subscriptions in COD-dominant markets; structurally, recurring billing needs a stored card, so classic "subscribe & save" is largely incompatible with a COD-first Bulgarian funnel.

### Cited Findings
- Supplement subscriptions run **4–7% monthly churn when well run and 8–12% at category average**, with **12–20% churning in month 1** **[BLOG – DTC agency citing Recharge/Ordergroove/ProfitWell, not independently verified]** — [EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/supplement-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark)
- Subscription take-rate of **40–70% for brands that default to subscription at checkout**; **45% of subscribe-and-save members keep it ≥1 year**; annual prepay retains ~2.5× monthly at month 12 **[BLOG]** — [EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/supplements-subscription-economics)
- Recharge publishes an annual "State of Subscription Commerce" report (primary vendor dataset) **[VENDOR]** — [Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/blog/announcing-recharges-interactive-2023-state-of-subscription-commerce-report/)
- In Bulgaria/Romania "over 60% of orders are paid at the door" (iHub citing Ecommerce Europe) **[VENDOR – BG logistics firm]** — [iHub](https://ihub.bg/en/articles/how-reduce-undelivered-parcels-e-commerce)

### Inferences
- Because most FitPatches orders are COD, a card-based subscription would only capture the minority paying by card. COD-compatible alternatives: multi-month bundles (the 3+1 tier is effectively a prepaid "subscription"), and replenishment reminders by SMS/Viber/phone around day 25–30 with a re-order discount (this is a standard practice, not evidence-backed here).
- Any subscription must comply with EU distance-selling and auto-renewal transparency; adding one also raises "subscription trap" scrutiny that the Digital Fairness Act is expected to target (see B2).

### Gaps
- No independent data on subscription adoption or LTV in COD-first markets (BG/RO/GR) was found.
- No evidence on whether Shopify subscription apps support COD recurring orders (assumed not; verify with the app vendor).

## A3. Money-back guarantees: effect on conversion and refund rates; length and wording

### Takeaway
Meta-analytic academic evidence shows lenient return/refund policies raise purchases more than they raise returns, with **monetary leniency (full refund)** having the strongest effect on purchase. Evidence on whether *longer* windows reduce returns is mixed in summaries. No rigorous supplement-specific data on 30 vs 60 vs 90 vs 365 days was found.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis of 21–22 papers (Janakiraman, Syrdal & Freling, *Journal of Retailing* 2016, 92(2):226–235) across five leniency dimensions (time, money, effort, scope, exchange): **monetary leniency (e.g., 100% vs 80% refund) had the strongest positive effect on purchases; effort leniency also increased purchases; exchange leniency reduced returns** **[ACADEMIC]** — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160620140944.htm); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Effect-of-Return-Policy-Leniency-on-Consumer-A-Janakiraman-Syrdal/e65cdc0e546317d8e71b9f7cc7fb98b2d6f09e6d)
- **Conflict on time leniency:** one summary of the same meta-analysis states that time leniency (longer window) **reduces** return rates via the endowment effect — [search summary / Adam Riemer](https://www.adamriemer.me/other/the-longer-the-money-back-guarantee-the-lower-the-return-rate/); the ScienceDaily write-up as retrieved says time leniency did **not** reduce returns as expected — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/06/160620140944.htm). Treat the "longer window = fewer returns" claim as unresolved.
- **60% of users expect return-policy information on the product page; 44% of sites don't display or link it on the PDP**; 15% of users have abandoned an order because of an unsatisfactory return policy (Baymard 2026) **[INDEPENDENT]** — [Baymard PDP](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux). Baymard's 2025 cart-abandonment list puts "returns policy unsatisfactory" at **13%** of abandonments — [Baymard cart abandonment](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)
- Academic work on MBGs' cognitive/affective outcomes exists (Suwelack, Hogreve & Hoyer, *Journal of Retailing* 2011) **[ACADEMIC – not read in full]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022435911000820)

### Inferences
- FitPatches' 30-day guarantee should be **visible next to the buy button** (Baymard), worded around full refund (monetary leniency) and low effort ("no questions asked, call/Viber us, we arrange pickup"), which are the two dimensions with the best purchase evidence.
- Legal framing: EU distance buyers already have a statutory 14-day withdrawal right (Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU), but sealed hygiene/health goods can be excluded once unsealed (Art. 16(e)) — so a voluntary guarantee covering opened product is genuinely additional value and can be marketed. Do **not** present the statutory 14-day right itself as a special perk (UCPD Annex I point 10 blacklists presenting legal rights as a distinctive feature) — [Directive 2011/83/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj); [Directive 2005/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj).
- Under COD, a guarantee mainly reduces *refusal at the door* anxiety; extending to 60 days is a low-cost test, but there is no supplement-specific evidence that it lowers refunds.

### Gaps
- No independent data on refund-claim rates for 30/60/90/365-day guarantees in supplements/wellness.
- No data on whether MBGs reduce COD parcel refusals.

## A4. Reviews: count, photo/video reviews, star distribution (4.2–4.7 vs 5.0), verification

### Takeaway
The strongest independent evidence (Spiegel/Northwestern, using PowerReviews data) says the first ~5 reviews drive most of the lift (+270% purchase likelihood vs zero), effects are bigger for higher-priced items, purchase likelihood peaks at 4.0–4.7 stars and falls toward 5.0, and "verified buyer" badges add ~15%. A perfect-looking or inconsistent review profile hurts trust and is legally risky.

### Cited Findings
- Purchase likelihood for a product with **5 reviews is 270% greater** than with none; displaying reviews raised conversion **190% for lower-priced** and **380% for higher-priced** products; **marginal benefit diminishes rapidly after the first five reviews** (Spiegel Research Center, 2017, using PowerReviews and Air Miles data) **[INDEPENDENT/ACADEMIC]** — [Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/); [PDF](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/04/Spiegel_Online-Review_eBook_Jun2017_FINAL.pdf)
- "Purchase likelihood typically **peaks at ratings in the 4.0–4.7 range**, and then begins to decrease as ratings approach 5.0" — [Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/)
- "**Verified buyer** badges ... improve the odds of purchase by **15%**"; anonymous reviewers are more likely to give 1–2 stars than verified buyers — [Spiegel](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/)
- PowerReviews benchmarks reportedly place the conversion sweet spot at **4.75–4.99**, with **perfect 5.0 products converting like 3.0–3.49 products**; 44% of consumers say the ideal average is 4.5–4.99 and 42% say 4.0–4.49 **[VENDOR data, reported via aggregator – primary report not retrieved]** — [eevy.ai](https://eevy.ai/blog/review-impact-on-conversion-rate-data); [PowerReviews guide](https://www.powerreviews.com/the-complete-guide-to-ratings-reviews/)
- Reported: user-generated imagery drives a **163.6% conversion lift**; shoppers interacting with photo reviews convert **91% higher**; 60% of shoppers "always" look for customer photos/videos; 62% more likely to buy if they can see them **[VENDOR/BLOG – aggregated, primary methodology unclear]** — [eevy.ai](https://eevy.ai/blog/review-impact-on-conversion-rate-data); [1440.io](https://www.1440.io/blog/how-reviews-impact-conversion-rates-a-data-backed-guide-for-2026/)
- PowerReviews has published research on review **volume and recency** effects **[VENDOR]** — [PowerReviews](https://www.powerreviews.com/review-volume-and-recency/)
- Baymard 2026: **89% of sites don't respond to negative reviews**; **63% prevent users from browsing reviewer-submitted images across reviews** **[INDEPENDENT]** — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Health & supplements stores with 100+ reviews/product reportedly outperform those with <30 by 40–60% CR **[BLOG – unsourced]** — [easyappsecom](https://easyappsecom.com/guides/shopify-health-wellness-ecommerce-guide)

### Inferences
- FitPatches' displayed counts (1,837 vs 1,012 on different pages) undermine credibility and are a legal red flag (B2). A single review app (e.g., Judge.me/Loox/Yotpo-style with post-purchase email/SMS collection and "verified buyer" badges) that shows one consistent count is both higher-converting (+15% verified badge) and compliant.
- Do not curate to 5.0: a natural 4.5–4.8 distribution with visible (answered) negative reviews is the evidence-backed sweet spot.
- For COD buyers without email, collect reviews by SMS/Viber link after delivery confirmation; photo reviews incentivised with a non-review-contingent thank-you (incentives must be disclosed).

### Gaps
- No independent study specific to Bulgarian consumers' trust in review widgets vs Facebook-comment-style blocks.
- PowerReviews 4.75–4.99 figure and UGC-lift figures could not be verified at primary source.

## A5. Countdown timers / scarcity: measured effect and backlash when fake

### Takeaway
Independent experimental evidence finds generic countdown timers and fake scarcity have **no significant effect** on purchase in controlled settings, while vendor data claims small positive effects for *genuine* deadlines and negative effects for fake/resetting timers. Regulators have repeatedly found fake timers on a large share of EU shops and treat them as blacklisted practices.

### Cited Findings
- Countdown timers implying a time-limited offer "**did not significantly increase consumer purchases**"; a scarcity condition ("only three left ... expires in the next 60 seconds") had **no significant impact** on acceptance (Luguri & Strahilevitz, *Journal of Legal Analysis* 13(1):43–109, 2021; nationally representative US samples) **[ACADEMIC]** — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jla/article/13/1/43/6180579); [PDF](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/national_meeting/Shining%20Light%20on%20Dark%20Patterns.pdf)
- Vendor compilation: across "18 published A/B tests", median lift **+9.1% for genuine deadlines vs −3.2% for fake/resetting timers**; claims 8–14% lifts in "controlled studies" **[VENDOR/BLOG – unverifiable compilation, low confidence]** — [LiquidBoost](https://liquidboost.app/blog/countdown-timer-conversion-data); [GrowthSuite](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-countdown-timer/psychology-of-countdown-timers)
- A 2022 Radboud University master's thesis studied the effect of deceptive countdown timers on buying behaviour and experience **[ACADEMIC – thesis, not read in full]** — [Radboud](https://www.cs.ru.nl/masters-theses/2022/J_Tiemessen___The_time_is_ticking:_the_effect_of_deceptive_countdown_timers_on_consumers'_buying_behavior_and_experience.pdf)
- EU sweep (Jan 2023): **148 of 399** online shops used at least one manipulative practice; **42 used fake countdown timers** — [European Commission Representation](https://cyprus.representation.ec.europa.eu/news/consumer-protection-manipulative-online-practices-found-148-out-399-online-shops-screened-2023-01-30_en) **[REGULATOR]**
- EU sweep (published 26 Mar 2026, Black Friday/Cyber Monday, 314 traders, 23 MS + IS + NO incl. Bulgaria): **18% used pressure-selling (countdown timers, "running out" claims) and over half of those claims were misleading or entirely fake** — [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eu-check-reveals-misleading-sales-practices-online-2026-03-26_en) **[REGULATOR]**; Bulgarian coverage — [Сега](https://www.segabg.com/hot/category-economy/onlayn-magazinite-masovo-mamyat-namaleniya-i-taksi)

### Inferences
- FitPatches' "изтича след 14:15:57" timer, if it resets per visitor/session (typical of Shopify timer apps), is (a) unsupported by independent evidence as a conversion lever and (b) a blacklisted practice (B2). Expected value is negative.
- Honest urgency that is permissible: a real dated campaign ("Есенна промоция до 15.10 вкл.") whose end date is real and not extended; real inventory-linked stock counters; courier cut-off timers ("Поръчай до 15:00 – изпращаме днес") that reflect actual dispatch times.

### Gaps
- No independent field experiment (real e-commerce traffic) isolating a genuine vs fake timer was retrieved. The frequently cited "~40% of timers are deceptive" figure (probably Mathur et al. 2019, Princeton "Dark Patterns at Scale") was not verified.

## A6. Mobile PDP UX: above-the-fold, sticky ATC, gallery, page speed

### Takeaway
Independent benchmarks show most mobile product pages are mediocre (62%), and speed has a measurable revenue link (Google/Deloitte: 0.1 s faster mobile → +8.4% retail conversions, +9.2% AOV). Highest-value fixes for a single-product COD page: speed, image gallery that answers "what is it/how big/how applied", return policy and total cost near the buy button, and a short form.

### Cited Findings
- Baymard 2026 Product Page benchmark: **62% of mobile sites have "mediocre or worse" PDP UX (only 38% decent+)**, vs 52% desktop and 64% apps; based on 30,000+ manually rated scores, 155+ sites, 115+ guidelines **[INDEPENDENT]** — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Same benchmark: **42% of users try to judge product size from images; 37% of sites lack in-scale images**; 81% omit price-per-unit for multi-quantity products; 67% lack total-cost estimate near the purchase section; 44% don't show return policy on the PDP — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Google-commissioned Deloitte study "Milliseconds Make Millions" (2020; 37 brands, ~30M sessions): **0.1 s mobile speed improvement → retail conversions +8.4%, AOV +9.2%; travel conversions +10.1%** **[INDEPENDENT consultancy, commissioned by Google; cited via secondary source]** — [Marc Radziwill summary](https://marcradziwill.com/blog/milliseconds-make-millions/); [Mesh summary](https://meshwithus.com.au/blog/site-speed-and-revenue-deloitte-gold-coast/)
- Baymard 2025 cart-abandonment: average documented abandonment **70.22%** (50 studies); reasons: extra costs 40%, delivery too slow 20%, didn't trust site with card 19%, account creation 18%, checkout too long/complicated 17%, returns policy 13%; better checkout design could lift CR **35.26%** for large sites — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate) **[INDEPENDENT]**

### Inferences
- Above the fold on mobile for FitPatches: product image showing patch in scale on skin + one compliant benefit headline + star rating with consistent count (linked to reviews) + price per day + delivery/COD/returns micro-line + primary CTA. Sticky ATC/"Поръчай" button is standard practice (no independent quantified study retrieved).
- Shopify themes with many apps (timers, fake-notification popups, multiple review widgets) are a likely speed drag — removing the timer and duplicate social-proof apps both reduces legal risk and speeds the page.

### Gaps
- No independent quantified test of sticky add-to-cart on mobile retrieved; Baymard sticky-ATC guidance not fetched.
- Deloitte primary PDF not retrieved directly (cited via secondary summaries).

## A7. One-page COD order forms vs standard Shopify checkout; phone confirmation and refused-parcel (RTO) rates

### Takeaway
Evidence that one-page COD forms beat Shopify checkout in COD markets is entirely vendor testimonial; independent support comes indirectly from Baymard's form-length research. For Bulgaria, logistics vendors report high COD share (>60% of orders) and 92–97% delivery success for supplements, with pre-dispatch confirmation calls improving delivery by ~4–7 pp.

### Cited Findings
- One-page COD form apps (Releasit, CodForm, EasySell) replace Shopify checkout with a single form; testimonial: "Adding a COD option with CodForm increased our conversion rate by 30%" **[VENDOR testimonial]** — [CodForm on Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/codform-1); [Releasit](https://www.releas.it/)
- Baymard: ideal checkout has **12–14 form elements (7–8 fields)**; average US checkout shows **23.48 form elements (14.88 fields)** **[INDEPENDENT]** — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)
- BG/RO/GR at top end of COD adoption, **40–60% of e-commerce parcels in some verticals**; Bulgaria: **92–97% successful delivery for supplements, cosmetics and food**, 88–94% fashion; Romania supplements 90–95%; pre-dispatch **confirmation calls give "a 4–7 percentage-point improvement on supplements campaigns"** **[VENDOR – 3PL BigArena, own parcel data, no third-party citation]** — [BigArena](https://bigarena.net/en/blog/cash-on-delivery-europe-guide)
- In BG/RO "over 60% of orders are paid at the door" (citing Ecommerce Europe); regionally **5–15% of orders go undelivered**; ~**20% of failed orders stem from order-data errors or poor communication**; proactive contact "can reduce non-delivery rates by almost a quarter" **[VENDOR – BG logistics firm iHub]** — [iHub](https://ihub.bg/en/articles/how-reduce-undelivered-parcels-e-commerce)
- With COD, RTO rates "double or triple" vs prepaid (5–12% prepaid) **[BLOG – India-centric sources]** — [ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/rto-in-ecommerce)

### Inferences
- FitPatches' name+phone call-back form is aligned with regional practice (short form, phone confirmation). The confirmation call is the right place for address validation and an honest bundle upsell, but scripted upsells must not be aggressive or misleading (UCPD aggressive-practice rules).
- Exaggerated page claims likely *raise* refusals: buyers who expected "−8 kg in 30 days" are more likely to refuse or claim refunds; compliant, realistic messaging is a plausible RTO lever (inference, not measured).
- Track RTO per ad/creative angle, not just CR — a page that converts higher on hype but has more refusals can be worse on contribution margin.

### Gaps
- No independent A/B data comparing one-page COD forms vs Shopify native checkout in Bulgaria.
- No independent Bulgarian data on refusal rates by product category; vendor figures only.

## B1. Omnibus price-reduction rule (lowest price in prior 30 days), strikethrough/compare-at prices; Bulgarian Consumer Protection Act and КЗП enforcement

### Takeaway
Any announced price reduction (strikethrough, "-50%", "Спестяваш X%") must be calculated from the **lowest price actually charged in the previous 30 days** (CJEU Aldi Süd, C-330/23, 26 Sept 2024). Bulgaria additionally limits a reduction to **between 1 day and 1 month**, so an evergreen "-50%" with a never-charged compare-at price is unlawful twice over. Enforcement is active (EU 2026 sweep: 30% of traders displayed discounts incorrectly; Bulgaria participated).

### Cited Findings
- Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161 inserted Art. 6a into the Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC: any announcement of a price reduction must indicate the prior price = lowest price applied in a period of not less than 30 days before the reduction; and amended UCPD penalties (for widespread/Union-dimension infringements, max fine at least 4% of turnover or €2m where turnover unknown) **[REGULATOR – legislation]** — [EUR-Lex 2019/2161](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/2161/oj)
- CJEU **C-330/23 Aldi Süd (26 Sep 2024)**: a reduction announced as a percentage or as a promotional statement stressing the advantageous price **must be calculated on the 30-day lowest price**; showing the 30-day low as a footnote while computing the % from a higher price is non-compliant **[REGULATOR – court]** — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/new-ecj-judgement-price-indication-directive-implications-online-retailers-and); [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/impact-of-the-cjeu-ruling-on-discount-advertising-in-europe-what-it-means-for-businesses); [Lawyers Week](https://lawyersweek.net/24305/cjeu-c-330-23-aldi-sud-a-price-reduction-announced-in-an-advertisement-must-be-calculated-on-the-basis-of-the-lowest-price-in-the-last-30-days.html)

### Inferences
- FitPatches' compare-at price is compliant only if that exact price was actually charged and is the **lowest** price in the 30 days before the sale, and the sale lasts ≤1 month in Bulgaria. A permanent "-50%" fails both tests. BGN 3,920,000 ≈ €2.0m (Bulgaria adopted the euro on 1 Jan 2026; fixed rate 1.95583); BGN 500–5,000 ≈ €256–2,556.
- "Спестяваш 66%" on a bundle: if computed versus an inflated compare-at price, it is a misleading price reduction; if computed versus buying the same number of single units at the **current** single-unit price, it is a price comparison that must clearly state the reference (6 in 10 traders in the 2026 sweep failed this). Safer: "3 кутии + 1 подарък" and "X € на ден" with no % claim.
- Compliant urgency + discount: run genuine, dated promotions (e.g., 7–14 days) from a stable regular price that was actually charged for ≥30 days, keep price logs (screenshots/Shopify price history) as evidence.

## B2. UCPD: fake countdown timers, false urgency, fake/unverified reviews, sweeps on dark patterns; Digital Fairness Act status (Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
False time-limited offers, fabricated or misrepresented reviews/social endorsements, and claiming reviews are from verified buyers without reasonable checks are **blacklisted** (always unfair) under UCPD Annex I; traders who show reviews must state whether and how they verify them. The Digital Fairness Act (targeting dark patterns, fake urgency, subscription traps) was still a planned Commission proposal for Q4 2026 as of the latest sources (Sept 2026) — not law.

### Cited Findings
- **UCPD Annex I point 7** (blacklist): falsely stating that a product will only be available, or available on particular terms, for a very limited time to elicit an immediate decision **[REGULATOR – legislation]** — [Directive 2005/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj)
- **Omnibus additions:** UCPD Art. 7(6) — where a trader provides access to consumer reviews, information on **whether and how** it ensures reviews come from consumers who actually used/purchased the product is material information; Annex I **23b** — stating reviews are from real users/buyers without reasonable and proportionate steps to check; **23c** — submitting/commissioning false reviews or endorsements, or **misrepresenting consumer reviews or social endorsements** **[REGULATOR – legislation]** — [EUR-Lex 2019/2161](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/2161/oj)

- EU reviews sweep (Jan 2022): of **223** major websites, **144** could not show they did enough to ensure reviews were authentic; **≥55%** potentially violated UCPD; **104** did not inform consumers how reviews are collected/processed; **118** gave no information on preventing fake reviews **[REGULATOR]** — [Commission press release IP/22/394](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/el/ip_22_394/ip_22_394_en.pdf); [ConPolicy](https://www.conpolicy.de/en/news-detail/misleading-customer-reviews-55-percent-of-websites-reviewed-violate-eu-law)

- 2026 Black Friday sweep: 18% pressure-selling, over half misleading/fake (see A5) — [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/eu-check-reveals-misleading-sales-practices-online-2026-03-26_en)

## B3. Health/medicinal claims: Regulation 1924/2006 (Art. 12 weight-loss rate/amount, professional endorsements), berberine status, Ozempic comparisons, Bulgarian authorities

### Takeaway
"Отслабни до 8 кг за 30 дни", "50+ диетолози препоръчват", a named doctor endorsement and "FitPatches срещу Ozempic" are each independently high-risk. For foods/supplements, Art. 12 bans rate/amount-of-weight-loss claims and individual health-professional recommendations; berberine has **no authorised EU health claim found** and EFSA (draft, March 2026) could set **no safe intake level**, with a possible EU ban/scrutiny listing pending. Because a transdermal patch is not a food, the bigger risk is being treated as an **unauthorised medicinal product by presentation**, which the Ozempic comparison amplifies.

### Cited Findings
- **Reg. 1924/2006 Art. 12** – not allowed: (a) claims suggesting health could be affected by not consuming the food; **(b) claims referring to the rate or amount of weight loss**; **(c) claims referring to recommendations of individual doctors or health professionals** and associations other than those in Art. 11 (national medical/nutrition/dietetic associations and health charities) **[REGULATOR – legislation]** — [EUR-Lex 1924/2006](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2006/1924/oj/eng); examples like "lose 10 kg", "reduce fat deposit by X%" are prohibited — [UK guidance on 1924/2006](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/nutrition-and-health-claims-guidance-to-compliance-with-regulation-ec-1924-2006-on-nutrition-and-health-claims-made-on-foods/nutrition-and-health-claims-guidance-to-compliance-with-regulation-ec-19242006)
- **EFSA berberine (draft opinion, March 2026):** "no safe intake level" could be established for berberine-containing preparations (Berberis, Chelidonium majus, Hydrastis canadensis, Phellodendron amurense); concerns: genotoxicity signals, carcinogenicity in rodent studies, idiosyncratic liver injury, data gaps; consultation deadline **4 May 2026** — [NutraIngredients, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/03/10/no-safe-intake-level-for-berberine-efsa-opens-consultation/) **[trade press reporting EFSA]**; EFSA's 2023 call for data on berberine — [EFSA PDF](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2023-07/call-for-data-berberine.pdf)
- Under Art. 8 of Reg. 1925/2006, listing in **Annex III Part A = prohibition**; **Part C = Union scrutiny with 4 years to submit evidence** — [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/03/10/no-safe-intake-level-for-berberine-efsa-opens-consultation/)
- Final EFSA opinion expected **early 2027**; at-risk groups flagged: children, adolescents, pregnant/breastfeeding women, diabetics, people with liver/heart disorders; 45 liver-injury cases linked to greater celandine — [NutraIngredients, 31 Jul 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/07/31/safety-in-question-what-is-the-future-of-berberine/)
- **ASA (UK, applying retained 1924/2006 rules) 27 Nov 2025 guidance:** GLP-1 production claims are medicinal and imply equivalence to prescription weight-loss injections; **"claims to curb cravings were also considered to imply that products had the same effects as POMs"**; "Faux-Zempic"/"OZEMP*C" comparisons prohibited; marketers "should not refer to the recommendation of health professionals" **[REGULATOR – UK, persuasive not binding in BG]** — [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/calorie-controlled-compliance-food-supplements-and-weight-loss.html)
- ASA Aug 2025: five rulings against supplement ads with unlicensed medicinal claims incl. "your all-natural Faux-Zempic!", "Natural GLP-1 Secretion Enhancer", "Unlike Ozempic, Curb Crave..."; ruling that a supplement "must not state, or imply, that [it] was equivalent to or had similar effects to prescription-only medicines used for weight loss, such as Ozempic" — [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/08/13/asas-ai-targets-faux-zempic-glp-1-claims-does-more-enforcement-mean-better-compliance/); [Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/asa-monthly-insights-august-2025-four-rulings-you-need-to-know-about); ASA Dec 2024 ruling on "ozemp*c dupe pills", "LOSE 15–20% OF BODY WEIGHT", "APPETITE SUPPRESSION" — [Aesthetics Journal](https://aestheticsjournal.com/news/asa-rules-against-ozempic-dupe-supplement-claims/)
- **Patches are borderline products:** classification as cosmetic vs medical device (or medicine) depends on intended use, claims and presentation **[BLOG – regulatory consultancy]** — [Certified Cosmetics](https://www.certifiedcosmetics.com/blog/cosmetic-borderline-products/are-skin-patches-cosmetics-or-medical-devices/); [EU Verify](https://euverify.com/resource/eu-cosmetic-borderline-products/); "little to no high-quality evidence" that weight-loss patches produce meaningful weight loss — [Second Nature](https://www.secondnature.io/us/guides/nutrition/weight-loss-supplements/kind-patches-review)
- **Medicinal product definition** (Directive 2001/83/EC Art. 1(2)): (a) presented as having properties for treating or preventing disease, or (b) used to restore/correct/modify physiological functions by pharmacological action; advertising prescription-only medicines to the public is prohibited (Art. 88) **[REGULATOR – legislation]** — [EUR-Lex 2001/83](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2001/83/oj)
- **Comparative advertising** naming a competitor/its trademark (Ozempic) must, among other conditions, compare goods meeting the same needs, objectively compare verifiable features, and not take unfair advantage of a trademark's reputation (Directive 2006/114/EC Art. 4) — [EUR-Lex 2006/114](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/114/oj)
- **UCPD Annex I point 17** (blacklist): falsely claiming a product can cure illnesses, dysfunction or malformations — [Directive 2005/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj); UCPD Art. 12: authorities may require the trader to furnish evidence of the accuracy of factual claims (relevant to "97% от жените...") — same source
- **Bulgaria:** Health Minister Katya Ivkova (parliamentary answer, 12 Jun 2026) on unregulated online trade in injectables/peptides incl. counterfeit GLP-1 agonists; medical specialists may not advertise medicines (Art. 246 Medicines Act / ЗЛПХМ); concern over "use of doctors' images, names and expert authority when advertising products with alleged therapeutic effects on social networks"; ИАЛ monitors websites, referrals to the Cybercrime Directorate **[REGULATOR]** — [Министерство на здравеопазването](https://www.mh.government.bg/bg/novini/parlamentaren-kontrol/4803)
- Reported: since start of 2026 ИАЛ carried out 450+ inspections, found 20+ illegal online platforms and 10,000+ unauthorised packs **[search snippet; source page not confirmed]** — [МЗ](https://www.mh.government.bg/bg/novini/parlamentaren-kontrol/4803)
- Food supplements in the EU may not claim to prevent/treat/cure disease (BG-language Your Europe) — [Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-requirements/food-labelling/supplements/index_bg.htm)
- A 2023 content analysis of EFSA's opinions on authorised vs rejected **appetite-related** health claims exists (most appetite claims fail substantiation) **[ACADEMIC – not read in full]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1756464623000713); EU Register of claims — [EU Register PDF](https://ec.europa.eu/food/food-feed-portal/backend/claims/files/euregister.pdf)

### Inferences
- Regulatory classification is the crux: a berberine patch is not a food, so Reg. 1924/2006 does not literally apply, and the authorised-claims register cannot be "borrowed" for it. If sold as a cosmetic, weight-loss/systemic claims take it outside the cosmetic definition (Reg. 1223/2009 Art. 2) and claims must still meet Reg. 655/2013 common criteria (truthfulness, evidential support). If presented as helping weight loss/blood sugar "like Ozempic", it risks being an **unauthorised medicinal product by presentation** (ИАЛ competence) — the most severe exposure. FitPatches should confirm with a BG regulatory lawyer under which regime the product is notified (CPNP cosmetic notification? БАБХ food-supplement notification? none?).
- Item-by-item: "до 8 кг за 30 дни" = rate/amount claim (banned for foods; misleading/unsubstantiated under UCPD; also a Meta timeline claim); "FitPatches срещу Ozempic" = implied medicinal equivalence + trademark/comparative-advertising risk; named doctor/dietitian endorsement = Art. 12(c) for foods, and in Bulgaria a live MoH concern; "50+ диетолози препоръчват" = Art. 12(c)/unsubstantiated; "97% от жените..." = needs a documented study (sample, method), otherwise misleading.
- Berberine-specific: EFSA's March 2026 draft and the pending Art. 8 decision mean claims about "safe, natural" berberine and any metabolic/blood-sugar claims carry rising risk; FitPatches should prepare for a possible EU restriction in 2027 (inference from process, not a decision).
- "Appetite/cravings" phrasing is **not** a safe harbour: no authorised appetite claim was found, and ASA treats "curb cravings" as implying POM-like effects.

### Gaps
- Whether FitPatches' patch is notified as a cosmetic, food supplement or nothing was not determined (outside scope).
- No БАБХ or ИАЛ enforcement case specifically against berberine or slimming patches in Bulgaria was found.
- EU Register was not machine-searched for "berberine"; statement that no berberine claim exists is based on absence in sources reviewed (verify in the EU Register web tool).

## B4. Meta advertising policies for weight-loss products (before/after, body-shaming, 18+)

## B5. What compliant, high-converting alternatives look like (mapped to FitPatches' current elements)

### Takeaway
Most of the conversion value FitPatches seeks can be retained with compliant equivalents that also have better evidence behind them: real verified reviews (+15% verified-badge effect, 4.x-star sweet spot), bonus-unit bundles with per-day pricing, genuine dated promotions, a prominent low-effort full-refund guarantee, and benefit language that avoids weight-loss amounts, medicines and professional endorsements.
